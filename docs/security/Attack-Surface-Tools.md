# Attack surface: Tools, System, Monitor and Dashboards

The Tools and System apps (Monitor is a section of System), load runs, component deploy and conf reload, hosted instances, the Monitor, Dashboards, report and CalcGrid grants, and jobs. The seed grants of `TOOLS_APP`, `SYSTEM_APP` and `MOQUI_API` are under [Parent frames](Attack-Surface-Web.md#parent-frames). This is one file of the attack surface map. The index, how to read a row, the defaults and the lock-down advice are in [Attack-Surface.md](Attack-Surface.md).

## Tools and System

`runtime/components/base/tools/MoquiConf.xml` mounts `dashboards`, `system` and `tools` under `apps`. The URLs are `/apps/dashboards`, `/apps/system` and `/apps/tools`, and the same names through the qapps extra path. Monitor is `/apps/system/Monitor/<Screen>`, and the two homes are `/apps/system/Home` and `/apps/tools/Home`; there is no `/apps/monitor`. There is no `/vapps` mount. `Tools.xml` and `System.xml` omit `require-authentication`, so both are required. `ADMIN` has inheritable always-all on each root, from the seed table above.

Tools' SQL, Service Run, Data Load, Data Snapshots, and `qzSign` are designed privileged tools. They, Tools' Data (browse, grid, export, documents), System's Resource and Audit and an app's administrator REST read every record whole, an app's confidential ones too (IronStone People's grievances); that is accepted, since the instance administrator's tools are privileged by design (the user's call of Nov 12, 2026), and an app keeps a confidential record out of its own and other apps' screens, search and notices instead. The elFinder screen was removed (Dev-AIDE replaces it); its commands are `/resources` on webroot. A screen-by-screen re-check of write refusals (the rest of the Java tool table) was not done. Screens another app shows ([Admin-Screens-Down](../../plans/archive/Admin-Screens-Down.md)). System's administration areas (Access, Security, Settings, Operations, Data, Audit, Integrations, Mail and protocols) and the Dashboards screens are also shown by another app under its own root: by location, in a wrapper, or inside a screen of the app's own ([Mounting the administration screens](../../runtime/components/base/tools/README.md#mounting-the-administration-screens), [Mounting the report screens](../tools/Monitoring.md#mounting-the-report-screens)). The rules a request meets there:

- Mount context is data. The mounting root sets `adminMount`, or `reportLinks` and `reportPolicy`, in its `always-actions`, and `System.xml`, `Dashboards.xml` and `System/Monitor.xml` set them to null, so a request parameter of those names is never read as one under either root. A value that is not a map is a mount with nothing in it: no route, no link, no feature and nothing of the instance administrator's. The map says where links go and carries the app's policy values; it opens no screen and authorizes no call.
- The instance administrator's parts (a job's, a group's, a remote's, a message type's or a message's record changed directly, a group of any id and type, the stored translation rows, artifact authorization, the whole audit log, the visits by user) are shown only with no mount, and only to a user whose own authorization records open System: `ec.artifactExecution.isPermittedOwn` (`moqui_core::user::permitted_own`) reads the user's own records and nothing from the stack, counts no hit and records no failure. The stack's question would not do: an app's inheritable always-allow on its area answers it for every user below it, and skips the transition, service and entity checks there as well. Every transition of such a part carries that question (`raw()` of `tools/template/AdminMount.js`) as its `condition`, so a post of one under a mount is refused, for a delegate and for `ADMIN` alike. A screen with such a transition is never mounted by location: an app's wrapper takes in transitions by name, and one that is not taken in does not exist on the app's route. `tools/test/AdminMounts.spec.js` fails on a transition of the areas that acts and neither calls only `org.moqui.admin` services nor carries `raw()`.
- No group of an app is granted a tools location: a grant there would open the screen under `/apps/system` or `/apps/dashboards` too.
- A policy value is enforced by the transition that reads the map, never a parameter, or by the service called. Setting an alert rule and sharing are refused first where the policy's feature is off (`ReportAlerts.requireSet`, `ReportShare.requireShare`), and making a playlist where the mount gives no editor (`createPlaylist` refuses when `reportLinks.definition` is null): the cases the engine cannot hold alone, since a holder of `save#Definition` need not be an author of the app. The people and groups a schedule or an alert rule names are asked of `find#ReportRecipients` under the policy's permission before the save. The email template, the alert topic and link and the recipient cap are written from the policy into every save, and the engine holds the cap under the instance ceiling. The wall display's `data` takes no range from the request. What a screen offers gives no right: the service asked decides.
- The report engine's own rules hold for a direct call too ([Business datasets](../tools/Monitoring.md#business-datasets), [Sharing](../tools/Monitoring.md#sharing), [Report delivery](../tools/Monitoring.md#report-delivery)). `create#BusinessDataset` asks the update artifact of the dataset named (`org.moqui.report.dataset.<id>`) beside the dataset-save artifact, so an id prefix a screen adds gives no right. `save#Definition`, and with it a copy, a restore, an import and a pull, takes a group only from a saver who belongs to it by a membership of their own (never `ALL_USERS`) or whose own grant holds `org.moqui.report.share.<userGroupId>` (update), and refuses any other (`SHARE_GROUP_UNAVAILABLE`); a group kept as stored is not asked again. `find#ShareGroups` names only such groups. `find#ReportRecipients` answers an account or a group only to a holder of the group delivery artifact, and accepts the caller always. `find#ScreenSchedules` and `store#ScreenSchedule` reach only the caller's own screen schedules. `get#BusinessDataset` answers a dataset the caller does not read as it answers one that does not exist.

The Tools database sections ([Database](../tools/Database.md), `crates/moqui-tools/`) add these rules:

- **Audit entities.** `DatabaseAudit`, `SqlRunToken`, `DatabaseConnection` and the `moqui.etl` mapping, value map and
  run entities are written only by their own services. The generic tools, entity REST and Service REST,
  request loads, ETL targets and every entity-auto service refuse them for a write, and the grid, Browse, Record, REST
  and ETL refuse them for a read (`AUDIT_ENTITY_KEYS` and `run_entity`'s check,
  `crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/sensitive.rs`, `crates/moqui-core/src/service/call.rs`). Tools › Data › Export,
  Snapshots and Browse's `export` read through the data writer, which is whole only for a holder of the sealed
  `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` and otherwise skips the restricted entities and writes no password or encrypted field
  ([Data export, snapshots and views](#data-export-snapshots-and-views)). An ETL `expression` runs as
  the mapping's user, so a generic write to a mapping would plant a script.
- **Views by origin.** `sensitive::is_restricted_entity_or_view` treats a view as restricted when any member is
  (`UserAccountAndGroup`, `UserGroupMemberUser`). `sensitive::secret_fields` follows each view alias to its member
  field, so an alias that renames a `UserAccount` password field is a secret too. The grid, ETL sources and lookups,
  entity REST finds and results, Service REST entity results and the JS `isRestrictedGenericEntity` use both.
- **No dummy parents in restricted entities.** A load with dummy foreign keys fails rather than create one.
- **External databases** keep the password encrypted, never return it, and clear it when the host, port, user, type or
  TLS settings change. An empty `sslMode` is `verify-full` for a host that is not loopback.

### Data export, snapshots and views

`Tools/Data/Export.xml` has three transitions. Each is `read-only="true"` with no `method`, so each takes its parameters from the URL or the body and runs on a GET, which is not token-checked ([CSRF](Attack-Surface-Web.md#csrf)).

- `EntityExport` (`authz-action="update"`) writes the records of the entities named in `entityNames` as XML, JSON or CSV. Its `filterMap` parameter is a JSON object of field values, parsed with `JSON.parse` and never evaluated (an expression there ran as a server script: plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-01, fixed). It is not `read-only`, so it takes a POST with the session token and refuses URL parameters; so do `exportDocuments` and `AutoEditMaster` `export`. `output=file` and `output=dir` write to the server path given in `path`; the path is not checked, and an existing file is not replaced (`File::create_new`). `output=browser` sends the text, or a zip made under `runtime://tmp/entity-export/<userId>/`.
- `DocumentExport.json` (`authz-action="update"`) sends one data document's records.
- `exportDocuments` (`authz-action="all"`) writes data documents to a server `path` or to the browser.

`Tools/Data/Browse/AutoEdit/AutoEditMaster.xml` `export` (`read-only`, `authz-action="update"`) writes one record and two levels of dependents to the browser or to the server file named in `filename`. That screen's `update` and `export` transitions call `WebUtilities.isRestrictedGenericEntity`. All four transitions use the data writer, which is full only for a holder of the sealed `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` (`ADMIN` in the seed): then encrypted fields are written decrypted and `UserAccount` rows carry their password hashes; for anyone else it skips the restricted entities and writes no password or encrypted field (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-05, fixed; [Export, snapshots and EntitySync](Attack-Surface-Data.md#export-snapshots-and-entitysync)).

`Tools/Data/Snapshots.xml` exports, lists, downloads, deletes, uploads and imports zip files in `runtime://db/snapshot/`. `exportSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) starts the `ExportEntityDataSnapshot` job; with no parameter it exports every entity to `MoquiSnapshot-<yyyyMMdd-HHmm>.zip`. `org.moqui.impl.EntityServices.export#EntityDataSnapshot` refuses a `baseFilename` that is not one safe path segment (SEC-TLS-07). `downloadSnapshot` (`read-only`, `update`), `deleteSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) and `importSnapshot` (`method="post"`, `authz-action="all"`) take a file name that must pass `WebUtilities.isSafeSinglePathSegment`. `uploadSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) keeps a `.zip` under the last path segment of the upload's name and replaces one of the same name. An import with `wrapForeignKeys` needs `DATABASE_ADMIN`. A snapshot taken by a holder of `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` holds every field of the entities exported, and one taken without it no secret, and an import does not set the loader's restricted-entity check; the writer, the service and the loader are in [Export, snapshots and EntitySync](Attack-Surface-Data.md#export-snapshots-and-entitysync).

`Tools/Data/Views/EditDbView.xml` writes `DbViewEntityMember`, `DbViewEntityKeyMap` and `DbViewEntityAlias` rows from `ec.web.parameters` (`field_<n>`, `functionName_<n>`); its `update` transition has no `method`. The Data Documents screens write `DataDocumentField` rows, which carry a `functionName` too. How such rows become an entity definition, and the check on a function name before it is written into SQL, are in [Entity definitions from database rows](Attack-Surface-Data.md#entity-definitions-from-database-rows).

### Service Run

`Tools/Service/ServiceRun.xml` `run` and `runJson` (no `method`, `authz-action="all"`) call the service named in `serviceName` with the request's parameters, in process, as the user, under the Tools root's inheritable grant. Nothing reads the service's `allow-remote` attribute ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)). A permission the service checks itself still decides. `run` writes each result value to the server log at INFO (`ec.logger.info`) and keeps the result in the session (`toolsServiceRunResults`) for the page after the redirect.

### Cache, threads, visits and print jobs

These System screens have no check beyond the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization.

- `System/Cache/CacheList.xml` clears one cache, the artifact caches or all caches (`clearCache`, `clearArtifactCaches`, `clearAllCaches`: no `method`, `authz-action="update"`). `CacheElements.xml` lists a cache's keys and the first 200 characters of each value, and removes an entry (`clearCacheElement`).
- `System/ThreadList.xml` shows every thread with what it runs, its stack and its trace id (`ec.factory.getThreadList()`).
- `System/Visit/VisitList.xml` and `VisitDetail.xml` show every user's visits.
- `System/Print/PrintJob/PrintJobList.xml` `downloadDocument` (`read-only`) sends the stored document of any `PrintJob` by `printJobId`.

### Transitions by method

Under `runtime/components/base/tools/screen`, 498 of 718 transitions have no `method` and 73 are `read-only="true"`; none sets `require-session-token="false"`. The rule both kinds follow (a GET is never token-checked; a transition with actions and no `method` runs on a GET with no query string; a `read-only` one also takes URL parameters) is in [CSRF](Attack-Surface-Web.md#csrf), and what a script run from one reaches is in [Who can author code that runs](Attack-Surface-Scripts.md#who-can-author-code-that-runs). The ones that were read:

- **No `method`, change state, need no parameter** (a GET with no query string runs them): `System/Cache/CacheList` `clearAllCaches` and `clearArtifactCaches`; `System/Conf/Changes` `reloadConf` and `restartServer`; `System/EdgeNode/Status` `unpair`, `unpairFront` and `drainSelf` (the service's `drain` parameter defaults to true); `System/EdgeClients/Domains` `recheckConflicts`; `System/Monitor/Settings` `revertAll`; `System/Llm/Usage` `rollupUsage`; `System/Llm/Skills/SkillList` `syncSkills`; `System/Print/Printer/PrinterList` `getServerPrinters`; `System/Integrations/SystemMessages` `sendAllMessages` and `consumeAllMessages`; `Tools/Data/Snapshots` `exportSnapshot`. `System/Data/Backups` `runBackup` is not one: `run#Backup` requires `reason`. Since SEC-WEB-04 was fixed (2026-11-16), such a GET that another site started is refused with 403 ([CSRF](Attack-Surface-Web.md#csrf)); the app's own links still run them.
- **No `method`, change state, need a parameter** (refused on a GET that carries one in the URL): `Tools/Service/ServiceRun` `run` and `runJson`; `Tools/Data/Snapshots` `deleteSnapshot` and `uploadSnapshot`; `Tools/Data/Load` `load`; `System/Cache/CacheList` `clearCache`; every transition of `System/Instance/InstanceList` and `InstanceDetail` that calls a service; `System/Operations/JobDetail` `updateJob` and `runJobNow`; in Monitor, `Settings` (`setSetting`, `revertSetting`), `Recovery` (`setStop`, `storePolicy`, `storeAdapter`), `IncidentDetail` (`updateIncident`, `confirmCause`, `rejectCause`, `addNote`, `approveDecision`, `refuseDecision`, `completeAction`, `cancelAction`, `revertAction`), `Incidents` (`createIncident`) and `ReleaseDetail` (`dismissRegression`).
- **`read-only="true"` with actions** (run on a GET with URL parameters): `Tools/Data/Export` `DocumentExport.json` (a download that writes nothing); `Tools/Data/Snapshots` `downloadSnapshot`; `System/Print/PrintJob/PrintJobList` `downloadDocument`; `System/Monitor/ReplayDetail` `events`. `Tools/Data/Documents/Edit/DataDocFields/FieldList` `createFieldDynamic` is not `read-only` (SEC-RTE-08): the field tree sends it as a `POST` with the session token and its parameters in the body (`screen/tree.rs` `loadMethod`), a `GET` with them in the URL is refused, and its service `create#DataDocumentField` needs create.
- **`method="post"`**: `Tools/Data/Snapshots` `importSnapshot`, and every writing transition of `System/Monitor/Checks` and `Sources`.

## Load runs

Tools › Load runs a load script on threads of the server (`crates/moqui-host/src/load_runner`, plans/archive/LoadRunner.md
§10; the guide is [Load-Runner](../tools/Load-Runner.md)):

- **Targets.** A run reaches this server (its own https URL, and its http URL only on a loopback host), loopback, and
  the URL prefixes of the `load_runner_targets` property; a dev instance reaches any URL. Plain `http` to another
  machine is refused unless `load_runner_targets` names it, since a login crosses in the clear; a prefix carries its
  scheme, and is matched on the path a request is sent to after `..` and `.` segments are resolved (`targets.rs`).
  `insecure` (an invalid certificate accepted) is a run setting shown on the run.
- **SSH.** A script whose plan uses `ssh` connects (`ssh.connect`, `test_client/ssh.rs`) only to this server's own SSH
  listener, loopback, and the `ssh://host:port` entries of `load_runner_targets`; a dev instance to any host. A host
  key not pinned by the script is taken only from loopback, or from any host when the run is `insecure`. It logs in
  with the account and password or key the script gives, so it can do over SSH only what that account may.
- **In-process service calls.** A script whose plan uses `service` calls this server's services through
  `service.call` as the user who started the run, with authorization required and the tarpit off for the run's
  contexts; a service that user is not authorized for is counted as an error and not run (D10; Java ran them as
  anonymous with authorization off). A load script's `service.call` goes straight to the service facade: the call
  runs in process as the user who started the run, with authorization required, the way the user's own screens call
  services, and nothing reads a service's `allow-remote` attribute
  ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)). A script's user threads have no
  `ec` and none of the server's own functions.
- **Secrets and files.** A `secret` param is held in memory for the run, never in the result, the snapshot or a log
  line, and is asked again on Run again. The terminal form names its field `password_<name>`, so a refused form's
  parameters, which the session keeps for the form shown again, leave it out. Run again of an imported run fills only
  the target kind, not the file's URL, params or settings. A run is read, deleted and imported only by its `runId`, a directory name
  under `runtime/tmp/test-load/`; an import is checked to be a result (16 MiB at most) before it is kept.
- **Limits.** One running load per instance (`load_runner_max_runs`) and 1,000 users a run (`load_runner_max_users`).
- **Demo.** The demo lock-down replaces `Tools/Load` with its Disabled screen.

## Component deploy and conf reload

System › Components and System › Conf ([Component-Deploy](../tools/Component-Deploy.md)), with the services in `org.moqui.dev.ComponentServices` (`crates/moqui-devkit/src/deploy/`). Each is reached under the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization, and each checks the `ADMIN` group itself; nothing reads a service's `allow-remote` attribute ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)).

- **What a load can do.** Loading a component, from a component directory or from git, runs its code on the server: its services, ECA rules, screens, REST APIs, templates and scripts are loaded, its data is loaded with authorization off, its tables are made, and its `MoquiConf.xml` is merged into the running conf. Treat a load as a code deploy. A component with Rust code (a `Cargo.toml`, or `.rs` files in `src/`) is refused: native code loads only at a start, when the binary links it.
- **Deploy from git** (`preview#ComponentDeploy`, `deploy#Component`, `update#Component`) also needs the sealed `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` permission. On a production instance it needs `component_deploy_enabled=true` too, and without that it is refused with a vague error.
- **URLs.** The URL must be `https` or `ssh` (also `user@host:path`), never `file://` or a local path. On a production instance a host that resolves to a loopback, private or link-local address is refused. The check (`check_url`, `crates/moqui-devkit/src/deploy/git.rs`) takes as the host the text after the scheme up to the first `/` or `:`, less anything before an `@`, so for `https://user:password@host/` it tests the user name. It resolves the name once, before the fetch resolves it again, and a name that does not resolve is not refused.
- **Fetching.** The database and bare storages fetch in-process (`moqui-git`). A working clone runs the system `git` command, with no prompt and the access token sent as an HTTP header rather than kept in the clone's conf. Both paths are refused in sim mode and by the `RemoteCall` fence. Credentials are stored encrypted (`GitRepository.accessToken`, or the `DeployedComponent` row for a clone).
- **Files written.** Fetched files go to a dot-prefixed staging directory that discovery skips, then into `runtime/components/addon/<name>` or the clone's component directory. Remove moves the directory to `.removed/` beside it; a working clone with changes asks again first. A tree fetched in-process is written by `write_commit` (`crates/moqui-git/src/checkout.rs`), which skips an entry named `.git`, `.` or `..` or with a `/` in its name, a link and a submodule, and sets no limit on the number of entries, the depth or the bytes.
- **Restart.** "Deploy and restart", "Update and restart" and System › Conf's Restart Now go through `restart#Server`: a dev instance only, `DEV_AIDE` or `ADMIN`, and a server that can restart itself.
- **Conf edit** (System › Conf › Edit: `get#ConfFile`, `check#ConfFile`, `save#ConfFile`) needs the sealed `CONF_EDIT` permission as well as `ADMIN`, since the conf names the code the server runs. A file is named only by its place in the merge, never by a path, and the default conf is never written. A save checks the file's hash from when it was read, writes the file whole and logs the user on `moqui.monitor.audit`. The merged-conf view (`get#ConfView`, `ADMIN`) masks secret values.
- **Conf reload** (`reload#Conf`, System › Conf, the dev file watcher, and `SIGHUP` when stdin is not a terminal) applies only the units the catalog marks live or apply. Ports, datasources, keys, `instance_purpose` and the other restart-class units wait for a restart. A property that the environment set is not overridden.
- **Cluster.** Only the database storage is fanned out, on the `component-deploy` topic (see Cluster below), and each member writes the tree from the shared database repository. The other storages, and a load from a local directory, are refused while the server has live cluster members.
- **Audit.** Each load, deploy, update and remove writes a `DeployedComponentHistory` row and a WARN on `moqui.monitor.audit`, as does a data load again (its history row only for a component a load recorded) and each conf reload request.
- **Attested deploys and the gate** ([Component-Deploy](../tools/Component-Deploy.md#the-gate)). `deploy#AttestedComponent` (REST `POST /rest/s1/moqui/components/deploy`) is the one deploy service with a REST route and no session: a source server's deployment posts an attestation (version 2: the target it is for, repository URL, branch, commit, component, the pipeline and run that passed, environment, deployment, data types, issue time) and its HMAC-SHA256 under the secret this server issued (`component-deploy` `attestation-secret`, at least 32 characters, from `runtime/.env`). The `component-deploy` element is read once at start (restart class: a conf reload or a live override changes neither the gate, the secret nor the allow list of a running server). The signature is checked first, in constant time over the exact text; a bad signature, and any attestation on a server with no secret, gets one generic refusal (an unauthenticated caller does not learn whether a secret is set) and counts as a failed sign-in for web protection. Then the age (`max-age-seconds`, 300; a minute of clock skew ahead), then the audience (it must be one of this server's `public-urls`, else the webroot URL from `webapp_http_host`; a server that cannot tell takes none, so an attestation for one target is not taken by another sharing the secret), then an `allow` entry naming the component, repository URL and branch (none: nothing taken; a secret alone does not choose what is deployed). Then, holding the component's cluster-wide lock (a `GitLease`), one conditional update claims the attestation on `DeployedComponentAttestation` only when the component took none issued at or after it (no replay, no rollback to an older attested commit, and the record outlives a discarded or removed component's row). It then fetches the branch (`https`/`ssh` only, as above) and deploys only that component at that commit, refusing when the branch has moved; a component already deployed from git is updated only from the same URL and branch. The data types loaded are the attestation's, signed; no request parameter widens them. The attestation stands for `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` for that one deploy (a thread-scoped grant cleared when it returns); `component_deploy_enabled` still applies in production. With `require-passed-run="true"`, `deploy#Component` and `update#Component` refuse any commit not so attested and `load#Component` refuses, so an administrator's session (or a stolen one) deploys nothing from System › Components; a start writes a database-kept component only at the commit an attested deploy put in place (never the repository's head), and another cluster member applies a published deploy only at the commit the last attestation names. **Threat model:** the gate keeps this server's deploy services to commits whose pipeline passed and (when the environment requires it, or the branch is unprotected) were approved on the source server, from the repositories and branches this server allows. It holds against a `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` holder of this server, an `ADMIN` who uses the deploy services (System › Components), a stolen session or API key of one, and a network attacker (the secret is never sent, a changed byte fails the signature, an old attestation is stale, a replay is refused, an attestation for another target is refused). It does not hold against whoever holds the secret, within the allow list: the source server (its database key and the environment's encrypted token; a compromised source or a maintainer who reads its database deploys what it signs), anyone who reads this server's `runtime/.env`; nor against whoever edits this server's conf (sealed `CONF_EDIT`) or files, who can turn the gate off or place a component for the next start; nor against whoever writes this server's database (an `ADMIN` with Tools' SQL and Allow Changes, Data Load or a record editor), who can change `DeployedComponent`, the attestation record or a component's `db` repository that a start or another member reads. A source signs only after the run passed, for a branch the environment allows, and after `decide#GitDeployment` by an approver other than the requester when approval is required; its run check still decides the run's trust. One secret per target still: the audience check stops an attestation for one target being taken by another, but two targets that share a secret share whatever one of them leaks.

## Hosted instances

System › Instance (`System/Instance/InstanceList.xml`, `InstanceDetail.xml`) starts and stops other Moqui servers as containers and makes their databases, through `org.moqui.dev.InstanceServices` (`runtime/components/base/framework/service/org/moqui/dev/InstanceServices.xml`).

- **Gate.** The screens are under the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization. Every `InstanceServices` service (not the CI `RunDocker` ones) refuses a caller without the sealed `INSTANCE_ADMIN` permission, granted to `ADMIN_ADV`, unless authorization is disabled (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-02, fixed); the screens call their checks only for a holder. The transitions have no `method` ([Transitions by method](#transitions-by-method)).
- **Rows.** The services read the `moqui.server.instance` entities: `AppInstance`, `InstanceHost`, `InstanceImage`, `DatabaseHost`, their types, and the env, volume, link and host-config rows. `InstanceDetail.xml` edits `AppInstance`, `AppInstanceEnv`, `AppInstanceLink` and `AppInstanceVolume` through entity-auto services. No System screen edits the others. `InstanceHost`, `InstanceHostType`, `InstanceImage`, `InstanceImageType` and its env, link, volume and host-config rows, `DatabaseHost` and `DatabaseType` hold the commands, templates and service names below, and are restricted for the generic tools, REST and request loads (`is_host_config_entity` in `crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/sensitive.rs`): seed data and their own services write them.
- **A service named by a row.** `init#AppInstance` runs, on a worker, the service named in `InstanceHostType.initService`; it and the start, stop, remove, check and database services a type names must be in `org.moqui.dev.InstanceServices`.
- **Docker Engine API.** `init#`, `start#`, `stop#`, `remove#InstanceDocker`, `get#DockerStatus` and `get#DockerLog` call the Docker Engine API through `call#DockerApi` (Rust, `allow-remote="false"`, `INSTANCE_ADMIN` unless authorization is disabled), on the CI driver's client (`DockerHost`), at `InstanceHost.hostProtocol` (default `http`), `hostAddress` and `adminPort` (default 2375). A host is reached over a unix socket, over https with the row's `tlsCaCert`, `tlsClientCert` and `tlsClientKey`, or over plain http only when it is loopback (`localhost`, 127.0.0.0/8, `::1`, `::ffff:127.x`); plain http to any other host is refused before anything is sent (SEC-OUT-08). The seed has an `InstanceHost` `LocalDocker` at `http://127.0.0.1:2375`. The container request takes the image, `Env`, `Binds` (`volumeName:mountPoint`), `Links`, `NetworkMode`, any `HostConfig` key from `AppInstanceHostConfig`, `Cmd`, and `AppInstance.jsonConfig` merged under it. `init#InstanceDocker` logs the image and the names of the `Env` entries and host settings, not their values (SEC-TLS-09). The request is also in [Requests the server makes](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md#requests-the-server-makes).
- **The registry token command is a conf entry the row names.** When `InstanceImage.authTokenCmd` is set, `init#InstanceDocker` calls `get#RegistryToken`, which runs the runtime conf's `instance-hosting` `token-command` of that `name` (split on white space, no shell, 30 seconds) and sends its first output line as the registry password; a value that names no entry is refused, so the row's text is never run. A registry login (an image with `authTokenCmd` or `password`) goes only over https, a unix socket or loopback http, as every Docker call does (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-03, SEC-OUT-08). `init#InstanceDocker` logs the image and the names of its env and host settings, not their values, and Service Run logs the result names only (SEC-TLS-09). The process is in [Child processes](Attack-Surface-Process.md#child-processes).
- **Row values expanded as script.** `create#AppInstance` expands `InstanceImageTypeEnv.envValue`, `InstanceImageTypeVolume.volumeName` and `InstanceImageTypeHostConfig.hostConfigValue` as `${}` templates with `ec.resource.expand`; a template is script ([Who can author code that runs](Attack-Surface-Scripts.md#who-can-author-code-that-runs)).
- **Database creation with an admin login.** `create#DatabaseMySQL` and `create#DatabasePostgres` connect to `DatabaseHost.hostAddress` with `adminUser` and `adminPassword` (an encrypted field; the seed row `LocalMySQL` has `root` and `moquiroot`) and run `CREATE DATABASE`, `CREATE USER` and `GRANT` built from `AppInstanceEnv` values. The names must match `^[A-Za-z0-9_]+$`. The password is put in a string literal with its single quotes doubled; a backslash is not changed.
- **Checks.** `check#MoquiServer` sends a GET to `/status` on `AppInstance.hostName`. `InstanceDetail.xml` calls `check#AppInstance`, `check#AppDatabase` and `check#MoquiServer` as it renders.
- **CI runs.** The pipeline run services of the same file (`start#RunDocker` and the others, `moqui_git::ci::driver::docker`) use the same `InstanceHost` rows; they are in [Pipelines](Attack-Surface-Git.md#pipelines).

### Monitor screens and telemetry

The receiver, RUM and metrics routes are rows of [Host routes and servlets](Attack-Surface-Routes.md#host-routes-and-servlets). What the Monitor screens and their services add:

- **What spans and logs hold.** A request's SERVER span holds the method, `url.path` (a token route's token left out), `url.query`, the `Host`, the client address, the user agent, the `Referer`, the route, the status, the first error message of the request, and the session's `session.id` (a digest of the bound session id, never the id itself, SEC-TLS-18), `user.id`, `moqui.visitor.id` and `moqui.visit.id` (`crates/moqui-host/src/http.rs`). It holds no request body and no form parameter. In `url.query` a value is `[REDACTED]` when `telemetry::sensitive_key` takes its parameter name as a secret: a credential parameter name (`password`, `api_key`, `login_key` and the others of `web::CREDENTIAL_PARAMETER_NAMES`), `code`, `moquisessiontoken`, `sessiontoken`, `sql`, `sharedsecret`, a name that contains `password`, `secret`, `token`, `authorization`, `cookie`, `credential`, `arguments`, `locals`, `api.key`, `api-key`, `apikey` or `private_key`, or one that ends in `_key`, `-key`, `.key` or `loginkey` (`crates/moqui-core/src/context/identity.rs`). Of the other parameters `url.query` and the Referer's query keep the name and, by default, the value only of `pageIndex`, `pageSize`, `orderByField`, `renderMode`, `lastStandalone` and `_uiType`, the others `[…]`, and the Referer no fragment; `<telemetry query-values="all">` keeps every value, for debugging (`http::redacted_query`, `redacted_referer`; SEC-TLS-18). A span attribute or a log attribute with a secret name is dropped. A log record holds its level, logger, thread, `user.id` and `moqui.visitor.id`, and a body of at most 16 KiB. The SQL text of a span and the pattern that masks a secret in a text value are in [Logs and spans](Attack-Surface-Data.md#logs-and-spans). The same rules run on received OTLP attributes (128 a record, 16 KiB a value) before indexing. The telemetry datasets have no row filter: a reader of `otel_spans`, `otel_logs` or `otel_errors` reads every user's.
- **Scrape targets and synthetic checks.** Monitor › Checks (`System/Monitor/Checks.xml`) stores `moqui.monitor.ScrapeTarget` and `moqui.monitor.SyntheticCheck` rows through entity-auto services. Both entities are members of `SYSTEM_APP`, so no permission beyond the group is asked. The jobs `scrape_TelemetryTargets_minute` and `run_SyntheticChecks_minute` run each enabled row whose interval has passed, and Scrape now and Run now call `org.moqui.monitor.TelemetryServices.scrape#Targets` and `run#Checks` for one row at once. A scrape is a `GET` of the row's `url` with `Authorization: Bearer` from `bearerToken` (an encrypted field) when set. A check is a `GET`, `HEAD` or `POST` with no headers and no body. The URL must be `http` or `https`. Both send with `deny_internal: false` (`crates/moqui-core/src/telemetry/scrape.rs`), so loopback, private and link-local addresses are reached ([The internal-address guard](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md#the-internal-address-guard)). The timeout is the row's `timeoutSeconds`, 10 by default and 60 at most. A scrape body over 8 MiB is refused after it is read, and at most 10,000 points a scrape are written to `otel_metrics` under `source.id` `scrape:<targetId>`. A scrape keeps only lines that parse as Prometheus samples, and the row's `lastError` holds the transport error or `HTTP <status>`. A check compares the status with `expectStatus` and, when `expectBodyContains` is set, searches the body for it; the row's `lastMessage` and a span hold the result.
- **Ingest keys.** Monitor › Sources makes an ingest key with `org.moqui.monitor.MonitorServices.create#IngestKey`, which needs `MONITOR_INGEST`. The token is `mqi_` and 32 random bytes in URL-safe base64 (47 characters). Only its SHA-256 is stored, as `IngestKey.keyId`, and a request's key is found by that hash (`crates/moqui-report/src/monitor.rs`). The key expires after `expireDays` (90 by default, 3,650 at most). The token is shown once, in a public message of the page after the post. `list#IngestKeys` and `revoke#IngestKey` reach only the caller's own keys: an administrator does not see or revoke another user's key there. A request is refused when the key is revoked or expired, when its owner cannot be signed in (`internal_login_user`), or when its owner does not hold `MONITOR_INGEST`. A token that is not `mqi_` and 47 characters is refused before any read. The key is taken only from the `api_key` or `Authorization: Bearer` header, never from the query.
- **What a key's sender sets.** The receiver forces `source.id` (the key's hash) and `source.name` (the key's label) on every record. Every other field is the sender's: `service.name`, `service.version`, `service.instance.id`, `user.id`, `moqui.visit.id`, `session.id`, `client.address` and any other mapped field are taken from the resource and record attributes (`crates/moqui-core/src/telemetry/ingest.rs`). The regression detector and the release list select by `service.name`, not by source (`crates/moqui-report/src/regression/mod.rs`). `POST /v1/annotations` calls `create_annotation` as the key's owner: the source is `deploy`, `config`, `release`, `user` or `business`, with `serviceName`, `instanceId`, `version` and `revision` from the body; `created_by` is the owner and `ingest_source` the key's label. A `business` annotation is taken when the owner's own grants hold `org.moqui.report.annotation.business` (create), which `ADMIN` does through `REPORT_BUSINESS_ADMIN`.
- **Runtime settings.** `System/Monitor/Settings.xml` changes settings in memory for 15, 60, 240 or 1440 minutes through `MonitorServices.set#RuntimeSetting`, `revert#RuntimeSetting` and `revert#AllRuntimeSettings`. Each needs `MONITOR_SETTINGS`, checked in the service (`crates/moqui-report/src/settings.rs`); `list#RuntimeSettings` needs only a signed-in user. A change needs a reason, is published on the `runtime-settings` cluster topic, is logged on `moqui.monitor.audit` and is written as a `config` annotation. The settings are the log levels (global, console, and a rule for any target; `trace` only on a target rule), `telemetry@sample-ratio` and `slow-root-ms`, spans and metrics per artifact type, the attribute list of each instrument (32 names at most), `telemetry/operations`, `telemetry/browser` (`enabled`, `replay-enabled` and the sample ratios), and `telemetry/receiver` (`enabled`, `requests-per-minute` up to 1,000,000, `body-limit` up to 64 MiB) (`crates/moqui-core/src/live_settings/catalog.rs`). So a holder turns browser RUM, session replay and the OTLP receiver on or off for a running server, and changes the receiver's limits, without a conf change.
- **Recovery.** A `RecoveryPolicy` matches an open incident by a service-name pattern and least severity, and its ladder names actions: `observe`, `escalate`, `pause-alert-rule`, `pause-job`, `disable-artifact`, `isolate-instance`, `pause-rollout`, `promote`, `restart`, `rollback`. The in-process adapter does four itself (`crates/moqui-report/src/recovery/execute.rs`): `pause-job` sets `paused` `Y` on the named `ServiceJob`; `pause-alert-rule` sets `paused` in the named report definition; `disable-artifact` creates an `ArtifactAuthz` row `RECOVERY_<actionId>` that denies the named artifact group to `ALL_USERS` for all actions; `isolate-instance` drains the named instance. `restart`, `rollback`, `promote` and `pause-rollout` are carried out only by a webhook or by an operator; no action runs a command. An action is started when a `MONITOR_RECOVER` holder approves its decision, or by itself under an `automatic` policy with `telemetry/recovery` `allow-automatic="true"`, and not while the emergency stop is on; `escalate` always runs. The seed policy `DEFAULT` (advisory) escalates major and critical incidents to the `ADMIN` group. `store#RecoveryPolicy`, `store#RecoveryAdapter` and `set#RecoveryStop` need `MONITOR_RECOVER_ADMIN`; approve, refuse, complete, cancel and revert need `MONITOR_RECOVER`; the incident and release services need `MONITOR_INCIDENT`. `run#Recovery` and `detect#Regressions` check no permission. The job `run_Recovery_minute` calls `run#Recovery`, which, with authorization off, advances each `RecoveryAction` row whose status is `Persisted`, `Running` or `AwaitingOperator` (`recovery/mod.rs`, `execute::advance`). Before each attempt it reads the decision again (`refused_by_decision`): it must exist, match the action's incident, action, target and adapter, and be approved (or blocked, for an action that changes nothing, escalation); else the action fails without running. While the emergency stop is on, an action that changes the system waits (SEC-TLS-11, fixed).
- **Recovery webhook adapters.** `store#RecoveryAdapter` refuses a `url` with credentials or a scheme other than `http` and `https`. It stores `statusUrl`, `urlProperty`, `secretProperty`, `headerPropertiesJson` and `allowInternal` as given, and the sender refuses a scheme other than `http` and `https`. The headers, the URL of `urlProperty` and the HMAC key are the values of process properties the adapter names (`moqui_lib::env::get_property`), each one starting with `recovery_` or listed in the conf property `monitor_recovery_properties`; a URL or header property the conf marks secret needs the list too (the HMAC key may be secret: it is not sent). The store and each run check this (`execute::property_allowed`; SEC-TLS-12, fixed). The route row is in [Host routes and servlets](Attack-Surface-Routes.md#host-routes-and-servlets).
- **Monitor rows and the generic tools.** The `MONITOR_*` permissions are checked in the Monitor services. The recovery entities (`RecoveryAction`, `RecoveryAdapter`, `RecoveryPolicy`, `RecoveryControl`), `InstanceDrain`, `IngestKey`, `ScrapeTarget` and `SyntheticCheck` are service-written (`sensitive.rs` `SERVICE_WRITTEN_ENTITY_KEYS`), so entity REST, Service REST, Tools › Data › Record, the grid, ETL targets and loads refuse to write them; they are written only by Monitor's services (SEC-TLS-11). A scrape target's or synthetic check's `allowPrivateNetwork` set to `Y`, or a move of such a row's URL, needs `MONITOR_PRIVATE_NETWORK` (`store#ScrapeTarget`, `store#SyntheticCheck`; [Outbound](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md)). An `IngestKey` row is accepted by its `keyId`, `revoked`, expiry and its owner's `MONITOR_INGEST`.
- **Session replays.** `System/Monitor/ReplayDetail.xml` `events` is a `read-only` transition that answers a replay's rrweb events as JSON. It and `MonitorServices.list#BrowserReplays` and `get#BrowserReplay` each check `MONITOR_REPLAY`. `get#BrowserReplay` writes one INFO line (`session replay viewed`, with the replay and the viewer) on the ordinary log target, not on `moqui.monitor.audit`; listing is not logged. The report engine refuses the `browser_replays` index as a dataset: by id, by `index:` alias and as the target of a saved dataset (`crates/moqui-report/src/lib.rs`). The server stores a chunk's events as the page sent them, after removing token-route tokens; masking is done only in the browser. The qvt client plays the events with rrweb's `Replayer`.

## Jobs

The scheduler is a thread in this process. `scheduled_job_check_time` is 60 seconds. The first job pass waits 120 seconds, then every 60. `0` starts no job runner. Tick hooks such as the cluster heartbeat still run.

Scheduled cron jobs do not log in a user and do not run with artifact authorization. That is the scheduler's default. `dispatch` leaves `user_name` empty unless the row is an overflow queue (`ASYNC_QUEUE_`). `run_job` calls `internal_login_user` only when that name is set, then calls the service with authorization disabled. `ServiceJobUser` is who gets the completion or error notification. It is not the identity the job runs as. An explicit run-now logs in as the caller, and a queued fire-and-forget call logs in as the original caller, and both still disable authorization for the call. The tx-commit rules the scheduler runs from the outbox (`ServiceOutbox`, written in the committing transaction) log in as the user who made the call and run under the authorization the call had when it registered them, as on the commit hook; no service or screen reads the outbox, whose rows hold the call's parameters and results. One job's error is logged and recorded. It does not stop the scheduler.

Two worker paths turn authorization back on as the user. `run#EtlMapping` calls `etl::access::run_as_user` (`crates/moqui-tools/src/etl/access.rs`): it requires authorization again and pushes the Tools screen, checked for all actions as the user, so the run's writes and service calls run the entity ECAs and the privileged-group and sealed-permission rules. The `DatabaseLoadWrap` job does the same from script with `ec.artifactExecution.runAsUser(location, body)` (`tools_host.rs`). A cron run has no user: `run#EtlMapping` then signs in as the saved mapping's owner for that one run, so the owner's own permissions decide. The owner must be a `ServiceJobUser` of the job. An `ADMIN`, who may write both `ServiceJob` and `ServiceJobUser`, may therefore schedule another user's saved mapping to run as that user; that is allowed on purpose. Either way the job's `location` and `contentBytes` are ignored, so the run reads only the mapping's own source. The owner's login gate applies (a disabled or terminated account, or a password that must change or has expired, refuses the run), and an inline mapping is refused; the load wrap refuses a cron run. `run#DatabaseJob` on a worker re-checks `DATABASE_ADMIN` and runs only server-made plans as raw SQL, with authorization off.

`System/Operations/JobDetail.xml` changes a job two ways. `saveJob` and `runJob` call `org.moqui.admin.OperationServices.store#Job` and `execute#Recovery` (the delegated path). `updateJob`, `createJobParameter`, `updateJobParameter`, `deleteJobParameter`, the `ServiceJobUser` transitions and `runJobNow` are the instance administrator's: each has the `raw()` condition, and they write `moqui.service.job.ServiceJob` rows, `serviceName` included, through the entity-auto services. `runJobNow` calls `ec.service.job(jobName).run()`. A run-now signs in the caller and calls the job's service with authorization disabled, so a holder of these transitions runs any service with stored parameters under no artifact authorization; a permission the service checks itself is read from the caller.
