# Attack surface: Tools, System, Monitor and Dashboards

The Tools and System apps (Monitor is a section of System), load runs, component deploy and conf reload, hosted instances, the Monitor, Dashboards, report and CalcGrid grants, and jobs. The seed grants of `TOOLS_APP`, `SYSTEM_APP` and `MOQUI_API` are under [Parent frames](Attack-Surface-Web.md#parent-frames). This is one file of the attack surface map. The index, how to read a row, the defaults and the lock-down advice are in [Attack-Surface.md](Attack-Surface.md).

## Tools and System

`runtime/components/base/tools/MoquiConf.xml` mounts `dashboards`, `system` and `tools` under `apps`. The URLs are `/apps/dashboards`, `/apps/system` and `/apps/tools`, and the same names through the qapps extra path. Monitor is `/apps/system/Monitor/<Screen>`, and the two homes are `/apps/system/Home` and `/apps/tools/Home`; there is no `/apps/monitor`. There is no `/vapps` mount. `Tools.xml` and `System.xml` omit `require-authentication`, so both are required. `ADMIN` has inheritable always-all on each root, from the seed table above.

Tools' SQL, Service Run, Data Load, Data Snapshots, and `qzSign` are designed privileged tools. They, Tools' Data (browse, grid, export, documents), System's Resource and Audit and an app's administrator REST read every record whole, an app's confidential ones too (IronStone People's grievances); that is accepted, since the instance administrator's tools are privileged by design (the user's call of Nov 12, 2026), and an app keeps a confidential record out of its own and other apps' screens, search and notices instead. The elFinder screen was removed (Dev-AIDE replaces it); its commands are `/resources` on webroot. A screen-by-screen re-check of write refusals (the rest of the Java tool table) was not done. Screens another app shows ([Admin-Screens-Down](../../plans/archive/Admin-Screens-Down.md)). System's administration areas (Access, Security, Settings, Operations, Data, Audit, Integrations, Mail and protocols) and the Dashboards screens are also shown by another app under its own root: by location, in a wrapper, or inside a screen of the app's own ([Mounting the administration screens](../../runtime/components/base/tools/README.md#mounting-the-administration-screens), [Mounting the report screens](../tools/Monitoring.md#mounting-the-report-screens)). The rules a request meets there:

- Mount context is data. The mounting root sets `adminMount`, or `reportLinks` and `reportPolicy`, in its `always-actions`, and `System.xml`, `Dashboards.xml` and `System/Monitor.xml` set them to null, so a request parameter of those names is never read as one under either root. A value that is not a map is a mount with nothing in it: no route, no link, no feature and nothing of the instance administrator's. The map says where links go and carries the app's policy values; it opens no screen and authorizes no call.
- The instance administrator's parts (a job's, a group's, a remote's, a message type's or a message's record changed directly, a group of any id and type, the stored translation rows, artifact authorization, the whole audit log, the visits by user) are shown only with no mount, and only to a user whose own authorization records open System: `ec.artifactExecution.isPermittedOwn` (`moqui_core::user::permitted_own`) reads the user's own records and nothing from the stack, counts no hit and records no failure. The stack's question would not do: an app's inheritable always-allow on its area answers it for every user below it, and skips the transition, service and entity checks there as well. Every transition of such a part carries that question (`raw()` of `tools/template/AdminMount.js`) as its `condition`, so a post of one under a mount is refused, for a delegate and for `ADMIN` alike. A screen with such a transition is never mounted by location: an app's wrapper takes in transitions by name, and one that is not taken in does not exist on the app's route. `tools/test/AdminMounts.spec.js` fails on a transition of the areas that acts and neither calls only `org.moqui.admin` services nor carries `raw()`.
- No group of an app is granted a tools location: a grant there would open the screen under `/apps/system` or `/apps/dashboards` too.
- A policy value is enforced by the transition that reads the map, never a parameter, or by the service called. Setting an alert rule and sharing are refused first where the policy's feature is off (`ReportAlerts.requireSet`, `ReportShare.requireShare`), and making a playlist where the mount gives no editor (`createPlaylist` refuses when `reportLinks.definition` is null): the cases the engine cannot hold alone, since a holder of `save#Definition` need not be an author of the app. The people and groups a schedule or an alert rule names are asked of `find#ReportRecipients` under the policy's permission before the save. The email template, the alert topic and link and the recipient cap are written from the policy into every save the screens make; the engine holds only the cap, under the instance ceiling: a direct `store#Schedule` takes any `emailTemplateId`, and a business alert's `audience` any existing template and topic. The wall display's `data` takes no range from the request. What a screen offers gives no right: the service asked decides.
- The report engine's own rules hold for a direct call too ([Business datasets](../tools/Monitoring.md#business-datasets), [Sharing](../tools/Monitoring.md#sharing), [Report delivery](../tools/Monitoring.md#report-delivery)). `create#BusinessDataset` asks the update artifact of the dataset named (`org.moqui.report.dataset.<id>`) beside the dataset-save artifact, so an id prefix a screen adds gives no right. `save#Definition`, and with it a copy, a restore, an import and a pull, takes a group only from a saver who belongs to it by a membership of their own (never `ALL_USERS`) or whose own grant holds `org.moqui.report.share.<userGroupId>` (update), and refuses any other (`SHARE_GROUP_UNAVAILABLE`); a group kept as stored is not asked again. `find#ShareGroups` names only such groups. `find#ReportRecipients` answers an account or a group only to a holder of the group delivery artifact, and accepts the caller always. `find#ScreenSchedules` and `store#ScreenSchedule` reach only the caller's own screen schedules. `get#BusinessDataset` answers a dataset the caller does not read as it answers one that does not exist.

The Tools database sections ([Database](../tools/Database.md), `crates/moqui-tools/`) add these rules:

- **Audit entities.** `DatabaseAudit`, `SqlRunToken`, `DatabaseConnection` and the `moqui.etl` mapping, value map and
  run entities are written only by their own services. The generic tools, entity REST and Service REST,
  request loads, ETL targets and every entity-auto service refuse them for a write, and the grid, Browse, Record, REST
  and ETL refuse them for a read (`AUDIT_ENTITY_KEYS` and `run_entity`'s check,
  `crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/sensitive.rs`, `crates/moqui-core/src/service/call.rs`). Data Load's render-time check
  passes every one of these lists and reads any entity back; its `storeSelected` refuses them for a write
  ([Data Load](#data-load)). Tools › Data › Export,
  Snapshots and Browse's `export` read through the data writer, which is whole only for a holder of the sealed
  `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` and otherwise skips the restricted entities and writes no password or encrypted field
  ([Data export, snapshots and views](#data-export-snapshots-and-views)). An ETL `expression` runs as
  the run's user, who is the mapping's owner on a cron run, so a generic write to a mapping would plant a script.
- **Password and identity fields.** Browse's `create` and `update` (`AutoFind.xml`, `AutoEditMaster.xml`,
  `AutoEditDetail.xml`) and Record's `update` pass the entity-auto service only the entity's fields the request has,
  without those `WebUtilities.isGenericWriteRefused(entity, field)` names: a password field, and `UserAccount`'s identity
  fields, which REST strips too. Those keep their stored values and are named in a warning: Browse and Record no longer
  set any account's `username`, `emailAddress`, `disabled` or password, and identity changes go through the account
  screens (SEC-TLS-81). The grid's save (`gridSave`, `grid.rs` `check_change`) refuses an existing account's identity
  fields with a field error, as it refuses the password fields, and an ETL target that may update an account (a store
  or an update, or such an entity-auto service) refuses them as target fields (`etl/mapping.rs` `validate`); a new
  account may set them in both. CalcGrid's `calcSave` saves only its own definition and writes no rows. `run_entity`
  strips nothing for the framework's own calls: the account services (`UserServices.xml`) write the same fields through
  `update#moqui.security.UserAccount`. A generic call's (Tools › Service Run, a `ServiceJob`'s run) refuses an account's
  password fields and an existing account's identity change (`account_write_refusal`, [Service Run](#service-run)).
- **Views by origin.** `sensitive::is_restricted_entity_or_view` treats a view as restricted when any member is
  (`UserAccountAndGroup`, `UserGroupMemberUser`). `sensitive::secret_fields` follows each view alias to its member
  field, so an alias that renames a `UserAccount` password field is a secret too. The grid, ETL sources and lookups,
  entity REST finds and results, Service REST entity results and the JS `isRestrictedGenericEntity` use both.
- **No dummy parents in restricted entities.** A load with dummy foreign keys fails rather than create one.
- **External databases** keep the password encrypted, never return it from their own services, and clear it when the
  host, port, user, type or TLS settings change. An empty `sslMode` is `verify-full` for a host that is not loopback.
  A connection refuses a loopback, private or link-local host; `tools@external-database-private-network` (default
  false) allows loopback and private, and link-local and metadata addresses are refused either way. The driver checks
  each address (`AddressGuard`), pgwire connects only to an address it checked, and MySQL tries each checked address
  by its text (`Endpoint::pinned`), except under `verify-full`, which keeps the host name for the certificate check,
  so the name may resolve again between the check and the connect (`crates/moqui-tools/src/ext.rs`, SEC-TLS-101). A
  connection's pool is kept in each process, keyed by the row's `lastUpdatedStamp` and `revision`, which the row read
  at each use is compared with, so a row stored or deleted on another cluster member is not served from an old pool.
  Data Load's check refuses a connection row, as a load does.
- **Raw SQL from scripts.** `ec.entity.runSqlStatement` (changes allowed), `runSqlUpdateConf`, `runSqlCountConf` and
  `testConfConnection` run only in a service's own code, in work with authorization disabled outside a screen render,
  or for a holder of the sealed `SQL_RUNNER_WEB`; a screen's actions, a REST resource or a web script is refused
  otherwise (`crates/moqui-core/src/script/host/tools.rs` `sql_allowed`, SEC-AI-102). No Tools screen calls them
  (`InstanceServices.xml` and mr-mantle's `PaymentMethodServices.xml` do), and they take no token and write no
  `DatabaseAudit` row. `runSqlUpdateConf` runs one prepared statement.
- **ETL** (`crates/moqui-tools/src/etl/`). A run is the user's, with authorization on and the Tools frame checked for
  all actions. A source is a file (the locations of the table above), a SQL source (an entity group or `ext:`, read
  only, with a `DbaSql` audit row) or an entity; there is no URL source. An entity source and a field's lookup read
  any entity that is not restricted, whole but for its password fields (by name or a view alias's origin) and its
  encrypted fields, and a source condition, a target's match field (`matchFields`, found with the condition encrypted) or a lookup's
  match or result field naming one of those is refused
  (`hidden_fields_of`, `Mapping::validate_with`); a holder of the sealed `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` reads the encrypted
  fields, as a full export does. `check#EtlSample` answers the rows to the browser. A target
  is an entity or any service whose verb is not `delete`, called once a row as the run's user. Only the owner or a
  `DATABASE_ADMIN` holder changes, copies or deletes a mapping or a value map, and whoever saves one owns it, so a
  scheduled run never runs as its old owner what another user wrote (`etl/service.rs:1021-1058`, `:1290-1293`). A run
  refuses a mapping whose value maps are not its owner's. A scheduled run signs in as the owner only when the
  `ServiceJobUser` row was added by the owner, or by an `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` holder whom `held_beyond` passes
  (`sign_in_owner`); `addedByUserId` is kept from the row's create on every update (`ServiceJobUserAddedBy`). Another
  user's mapping is scheduled only with `DATABASE_ADMIN`, `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` and no access the owner holds beyond the
  caller, as the owner's job (SEC-TLS-103).
  `get#EtlMapping` answers any saved mapping, its `sourceSql` and `expression` texts included, to a Tools viewer, and
  `Etl/Mappings.xml` and `Etl/ValueMaps.xml` list every user's. A run's error rows (`EtlRunError.rowJson`) and error
  file (`dbresource://moqui/etl-run/<userId>/<runId>/errors.csv`) keep source rows and are read by the run's user or a
  `DATABASE_ADMIN` holder. Every write of the ETL screens is a POST with `authz-action="all"` and an explicit parameter
  map.
- **The database audit** (`DatabaseAudit`). Every statement of a run with changes allowed is audited `isChange` Y, an
  EXPLAIN holding ANALYZE is a change (`plain_read`), and a refusal after the token is taken is audited. A
  connection's store, delete and test write `DbaConnection` rows, a refusal of a non-owner included; a grid page of an
  entity source writes `DbaGridRead` and every grid export `DbaExport` (`grid::audit_read`; SEC-TLS-102). It is not
  proof against a SQL holder: a run with changes can change the audit table, which is in the same database.

### Data export, snapshots and views

`Tools/Data/Export.xml` has three transitions. Each is `read-only="true"` with no `method`, so each takes its parameters from the URL or the body and runs on a GET, which is not token-checked ([CSRF](Attack-Surface-Web.md#csrf)).

- `EntityExport` (`authz-action="update"`) writes the records of the entities named in `entityNames` as XML, JSON or CSV. Its `filterMap` parameter is a JSON object of field values, parsed with `JSON.parse` and never evaluated (an expression there ran as a server script: plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-01, fixed). An `orderBy` entry that is not a field of the entity is dropped before SQL (`crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/find.rs`). Neither `filterMap` nor `orderBy` keeps out a password or encrypted field, so for a caller without `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` the find takes such a condition though the rows leave the field out. It is not `read-only`, so it takes a POST with the session token and refuses URL parameters; so do `exportDocuments` and `AutoEditMaster` `export`. `output=file` and `output=dir` write to the server path given in `path`; the path is not checked, and an existing file is not replaced (`File::create_new`). `output=browser` sends the text, or a zip made under `runtime://tmp/entity-export/<userId>/`.
- `DocumentExport.json` (`authz-action="update"`) sends one data document's records.
- `exportDocuments` (`authz-action="all"`) writes data documents to a server `path` or to the browser.

`Tools/Data/Browse/AutoEdit/AutoEditMaster.xml` `export` (`read-only`, `authz-action="update"`) writes one record and two levels of dependents to the browser or to the server file named in `filename`. That screen's `update` and `export` transitions call `WebUtilities.isRestrictedGenericEntity`. All four transitions use the data writer, which is full only for a holder of the sealed `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` (`ADMIN` in the seed): then encrypted fields are written decrypted and `UserAccount` rows carry their password hashes; for anyone else it skips the restricted entities and writes no password or encrypted field (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-05, fixed; [Export, snapshots and EntitySync](Attack-Surface-Data.md#export-snapshots-and-entitysync)).

`Tools/Data/Snapshots.xml` exports, lists, downloads, deletes, uploads and imports zip files in `runtime://db/snapshot/`. `exportSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) starts the `ExportEntityDataSnapshot` job; with no parameter it exports every entity to `MoquiSnapshot-<yyyyMMdd-HHmm>.zip`. `org.moqui.impl.EntityServices.export#EntityDataSnapshot` refuses a `baseFilename` that is not one safe path segment (SEC-TLS-07). `downloadSnapshot` (`read-only`, `update`), `deleteSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) and `importSnapshot` (`method="post"`, `authz-action="all"`) take a file name that must pass `WebUtilities.isSafeSinglePathSegment`. `uploadSnapshot` (no `method`, `update`) keeps a `.zip` under the last path segment of the upload's name and replaces one of the same name. An import with `wrapForeignKeys` needs `DATABASE_ADMIN`. A snapshot taken by a holder of `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` holds every field of the entities exported, and one taken without it no secret, and an import does not set the loader's restricted-entity check; the writer, the service and the loader are in [Export, snapshots and EntitySync](Attack-Surface-Data.md#export-snapshots-and-entitysync).

`Tools/Data/Views/EditDbView.xml` writes `DbViewEntityMember`, `DbViewEntityKeyMap` and `DbViewEntityAlias` rows from `ec.web.parameters` (`field_<n>`, `functionName_<n>`); its `update` transition has no `method`. The Data Documents screens write `DataDocumentField` rows, which carry a `functionName` too. How such rows become an entity definition, and the check on a function name before it is written into SQL, are in [Entity definitions from database rows](Attack-Surface-Data.md#entity-definitions-from-database-rows).

### Service Run

`Tools/Service/ServiceRun.xml` `run` and `runJson` (no `method`, `authz-action="all"`) call the service named in `serviceName` with the request's parameters, in process, as the user, under the Tools root's inheritable grant. Nothing reads the service's `allow-remote` attribute ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)). A permission the service checks itself still decides. `run` writes each result value to the server log at INFO (`ec.logger.info`) and keeps the result in the session (`toolsServiceRunResults`) for the page after the redirect. Both call with `generic()`, so an entity-auto write, and each record nested in it, is refused where a generic write is (`run_entity`, `crates/moqui-core/src/service/call.rs`): an entity a generic write refuses (the identity-admin, audit, host-configuration, service-written and service job entities; a secret-config one but for a member of `ADMIN`; a mail account's own server; `generic_entity_refusal`, the rule Service REST's entity resources call too), and a `UserAccount` write that sets a password field or changes an existing account's identity field (`account_write_refusal`, as the loader's `protectUserAccounts`). A defined service that writes one runs with its own checks, and so do the entity-auto calls it makes. Services no screen calls are reached this way too: `org.moqui.resource.SpreadsheetServices.calculate#Spreadsheet` reads any resource `location` and writes the calculated workbook to any `outputLocation`, checking neither (`crates/moqui-core/src/service/spreadsheet.rs`, `spreadsheet_calc.rs`); `backup#EmbeddedDatabase` with a `backupDir` and a mirror remote of the caller's choice ([The embedded database backup](#the-embedded-database-backup)); `pull#DefinitionGitSyncs`, which checks no permission.

### Data Load

`Tools/Data/Load.xml` loads entity data from text or a server location (`crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/load_feed/`).

- `load` (`authz-action="all"`) loads `xmlText`, `jsonText`, `csvText` or a `location` with `restrictSensitiveEntities(true)`: the loader refuses the `is_generic_write_refused` entities and every service element. It also sets `protectUserAccounts(true)`, as the load wrap's job (`run#LoadWrap`) does, so a `UserAccount` record that sets `currentPassword`, `passwordSalt` or another password field is refused, and so is one that changes an identity field (`emailAddress`, `username`, `disabled` and the rest) of an account that is there, which a password reset would then hand over (SEC-TLS-81); a new account may be loaded with them. The seed, component and EntitySync loads set their own options.
- `storeSelected` (`method="post"`, `authz-action="all"`, as `load`) takes `rowOps`, a list of base64url JSON objects that each name an `entity`, a `pk` and a `field` and `value` or `createValues`, and writes each with `ec.entity.makeValue(...)` and `createOrUpdate()` or `create()`. The screen makes the list from a check's rows, but nothing ties a posted row to a check (the holder chooses the check's data anyway), so the rule is on the row: a row whose entity `WebUtilities.isGenericWriteRefused` names (the identity, secret-config, audit, hosted-instance and service-written entities, the list `load` refuses) or that sets a field `isGenericWriteRefused(entity, field)` names (a password field, and `UserAccount`'s identity fields: `username`, `emailAddress`, `externalAuthId`, `disabled`, `ipAllowed`, `requirePasswordChange` and the rest; in its value or its `pk` map) is not written and is named in a warning; the other rows are written (SEC-TLS-81, `tools/test/DataLoadStore.spec.js`).
- The check (`runCheck=true` on the screen, or `load` with `checkOnly`) runs as the screen renders, and only for a user authorized for all actions on the screen, as `load` is (`isPermitted`, `Load.xml`). The loader refuses the restricted entities before it compares, as a load does, and a check refuses an entity or view a generic read refuses and compares no password or encrypted field (`check_tree`, `load_feed/check.rs`): for each other field whose given value differs the form shows `dbValue`, the stored value as the entity facade returns it, and for a record not found every value given. A `location` is a local path (`classpath:`, `component://` with `..` kept, `file:`, or any host path; a directory loads every `.xml`, `.csv`, `.json` and `.zip` in it) and is never fetched, so the `https?:` test of `load` changes nothing for it. A file that is not entity data yields nothing as XML, and as CSV or JSON its first cell, header names or `_entity` value are echoed in an error; other extensions are refused. A file that is entity data, a snapshot on disk for example, is compared with the database.

### Cache, threads, visits and print jobs

These System screens have no check beyond the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization.

- `System/Visit/VisitList.xml` and `VisitDetail.xml` show every user's visits.

## Load runs

Tools › Load runs a load script on threads of the server (`crates/moqui-host/src/load_runner`, plans/archive/LoadRunner.md
§10; the guide is [Load-Runner](../tools/Load-Runner.md)):

- **Targets.** A run reaches this server (its own https URL, and its http URL only on a loopback host), loopback, and
  the URL prefixes of the `load-runner@targets` property; a dev instance reaches any URL. Plain `http` to another
  machine is refused unless `load-runner@targets` names it, since a login crosses in the clear; a prefix carries its
  scheme, and is matched on the path a request is sent to after `..` and `.` segments are resolved (`targets.rs`), on
  a segment boundary. A target with user info is refused, loopback is taken by literal address only, and this server's
  own origin only over https. Each request and each SSH connect asks the policy, and the client follows no redirect by
  itself (`test_client/mod.rs`).
  `insecure` (an invalid certificate accepted) is a run setting shown on the run.
- **SSH.** A script whose plan uses `ssh` connects (`ssh.connect`, `test_client/ssh.rs`) only to this server's own SSH
  listener, loopback, and the `ssh://host:port` entries of `load-runner@targets`; a dev instance to any host. A host
  key not pinned by the script is taken only from loopback, or from any host when the run is `insecure`. It logs in
  with the account and password or key the script gives, so it can do over SSH only what that account may.
- **In-process service calls.** A script whose plan uses `service` calls this server's services through
  `service.call` as the user who started the run, with authorization required and the tarpit off for the run's
  contexts; a service that user is not authorized for is counted as an error and not run (D10; Java ran them as
  anonymous with authorization off). A load script's `service.call` goes straight to the service facade: the call
  runs in process as the user who started the run, with authorization required, the way the user's own screens call
  services, and nothing reads a service's `allow-remote` attribute
  ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)). A script's user threads have no
  `ec` and none of the server's own functions.
- **Secrets and files.** A `secret` param is held in memory for the run, never in the result, the snapshot or a log
  line, and is asked again on Run again. The terminal form names its field `password_<name>`, so a refused form's
  parameters, which the session keeps for the form shown again, leave it out. Run again of an imported run fills only
  the target kind, not the file's URL, params or settings. A run is read, deleted and imported only by its `runId`, a directory name
  under `runtime/tmp/test-load/`; an import is checked to be a result (16 MiB at most) before it is kept. A run names
  a script only as `component://<component>/test/load/<Name>.load.js`, the name letters, digits, `_`, `-` and `.`
  with no `..` and no leading dot (`scripts.rs`), and a script's `files` calls read only inside its own component
  directory (`test_client/mod.rs` `confined`).
- **Limits.** One running load per instance (`load-runner@max-runs`) and 1,000 users a run (`load-runner@max-users`).
- **Demo.** The demo lock-down replaces `Tools/Load` with its Disabled screen.

## Component deploy and conf reload

System › Components and System › Conf ([Component-Deploy](../tools/Component-Deploy.md)), with the services in `org.moqui.dev.ComponentServices` (`crates/moqui-devkit/src/deploy/`). Each is reached under the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization. `load#Component` and `remove#Component` check the `ADMIN` group themselves, and the conf services as below; the five git services (`preview#ComponentDeploy`, `deploy#Component`, `discard#ComponentDeploy`, `preview#ComponentUpdate`, `update#Component`) check the sealed `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` (`gate()`, `crates/moqui-devkit/src/deploy/git.rs`), not the group. Nothing reads a service's `allow-remote` attribute ([Services and `allow-remote`](Attack-Surface-Web.md#services-and-allow-remote)).

- **What a load can do.** Loading a component, from a component directory or from git, runs its code on the server: its services, ECA rules, screens, REST APIs, templates and scripts are loaded, its data is loaded with authorization off, its tables are made, and its `MoquiConf.xml` is merged into the running conf. Treat a load as a code deploy. A component with Rust code (a `Cargo.toml`, or `.rs` files in `src/`) is refused: native code loads only at a start, when the binary links it. `load#Component` reads a component only from the conf's component directories, by name, and needs the `ADMIN` group only; it loads the component's data with authorization off and no restricted-entity check.
- **Deploy from git** (`preview#ComponentDeploy`, `deploy#Component`, `update#Component`) needs the sealed `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` permission. On a production instance it needs `moqui_conf__component_deploy__enabled=true` too, and without that it is refused with a vague error. `preview#ComponentDeploy` writes before anything is deployed and is not held by `require-passed-run`: it creates or overwrites the `GitRepository` row `component-<name>` with the request's URL, user name and token, imports the branch into it or replaces `runtime/git/component-<name>`, and writes the tree to `.staging-<name>`; the check that the name is already deployed comes after.
- **URLs.** The URL must be `https` or `ssh` (also `user@host:path`), never `file://` or a local path. The check (`check_url`, `crates/moqui-devkit/src/deploy/git.rs`) parses the URL with the `url` crate. On a production instance a host that does not resolve, or that resolves to a loopback, private or link-local address, is refused, and the fetch connects only to an address it checked: in process under `moqui_git::deny_internal`, and the system `git` by an address pin (`http.curloptResolve` over https, a pinned `core.sshCommand` over ssh) (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-08, fixed). On another instance nothing is refused.
- **Fetching.** The database and bare storages fetch in-process (`moqui-git`). A working clone runs the system `git` command (`clone --branch <b> --single-branch -- <url>`, `fetch origin`, `rev-parse`, `status`, `merge-base`, `diff --name-only`, `merge --ff-only`; the URL after `--`), with no prompt and the access token sent as an HTTP header rather than kept in the clone's conf. Both paths are refused in sim mode and by the `RemoteCall` fence. Credentials are stored encrypted (`GitRepository.accessToken`, or the `DeployedComponent` row for a clone).
- **Files written.** Fetched files go to a dot-prefixed staging directory that discovery skips, then into `runtime/components/addon/<name>` or the clone's component directory. Remove moves the directory to `.removed/` beside it; a working clone with changes asks again first. Remove, deploy and discard take the directory from the `DeployedComponent` row's `location`: remove renames that directory to `.removed/<name>-<millis>` beside it, and deploy and discard rename or delete `.staging-<name>` beside it, with `componentName` from the request and no name check in these three. A tree fetched in-process is written by `write_commit` (`crates/moqui-git/src/checkout.rs`), which skips an entry named `.git`, `.` or `..` or with a `/` in its name, a link and a submodule, and sets no limit on the number of entries, the depth or the bytes.
- **Restart.** "Deploy and restart", "Update and restart" and System › Conf's Restart Now go through `restart#Server`: a dev instance only, `DEV_AIDE` or `ADMIN`, and a server that can restart itself.
- **Conf edit** (System › Conf › Edit: `get#ConfFile`, `check#ConfFile`, `save#ConfFile`) needs the sealed `CONF_EDIT` permission as well as `ADMIN`, since the conf names the code the server runs. A file is named only by its place in the merge, never by a path, and the default conf is never written. A save checks the file's hash from when it was read, writes the file whole and logs the user on `moqui.monitor.audit`. The merged-conf view (`get#ConfView`, `ADMIN`) masks secret values.
- **Conf reload** (`reload#Conf`, System › Conf, the dev file watcher, and `SIGHUP` when stdin is not a terminal) applies only the units the catalog marks live or apply. Ports, datasources, keys, `instance@purpose` and the other restart-class units wait for a restart. A property that the environment set is not overridden.
- **Cluster.** Only the database storage is fanned out, on the `component-deploy` topic (see Cluster below), and each member writes the tree from the shared database repository. The other storages, and a load from a local directory, are refused while the server has live cluster members.
- **Audit.** Each load, deploy, update and remove writes a `DeployedComponentHistory` row and a WARN on `moqui.monitor.audit`, as does a data load again (its history row only for a component a load recorded) and each conf reload request.
- **Attested deploys and the gate** ([Component-Deploy](../tools/Component-Deploy.md#the-gate)). `deploy#AttestedComponent` (REST `POST /rest/s1/moqui/components/deploy`) is the one deploy service with a REST route and no session: a source server's deployment posts an attestation (version 2: the target it is for, repository URL, branch, commit, component, the pipeline and run that passed, environment, deployment, data types, issue time) and its HMAC-SHA256 under the secret this server issued (`component-deploy` `attestation-secret`, at least 32 characters, from `runtime/.env`). The `component-deploy` element is read once at start (restart class: a conf reload or a live override changes neither the gate, the secret nor the allow list of a running server). The signature is checked first, in constant time over the exact text; a bad signature, and any attestation on a server with no secret, gets one generic refusal (an unauthenticated caller does not learn whether a secret is set) and counts as a failed sign-in for web protection. Then the age (`max-age-seconds`, 300; a minute of clock skew ahead), then the audience (it must be one of this server's `public-urls`, else the webroot URL from `webapp-list/webapp[webroot]@http-host`; a server that cannot tell takes none, so an attestation for one target is not taken by another sharing the secret), then an `allow` entry naming the component, repository URL and branch (none: nothing taken; a secret alone does not choose what is deployed). Then, holding the component's cluster-wide lock (a `GitLease`), one conditional update claims the attestation on `DeployedComponentAttestation` only when the component took none issued at or after it (no replay, no rollback to an older attested commit, and the record outlives a discarded or removed component's row). It then fetches the branch (`https`/`ssh` only, as above) and deploys only that component at that commit, refusing when the branch has moved; a component already deployed from git is updated only from the same URL and branch. The data types loaded are the attestation's, signed; no request parameter widens them. The attestation stands for `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` for that one deploy (a thread-scoped grant cleared when it returns); `component-deploy@enabled` still applies in production. With `require-passed-run="true"`, `deploy#Component` and `update#Component` refuse any commit not so attested and `load#Component` refuses, so an administrator's session (or a stolen one) deploys nothing from System › Components; a start writes a database-kept component only at the commit an attested deploy put in place (never the repository's head), and another cluster member applies a published deploy only at the commit the last attestation names. **Threat model:** the gate keeps this server's deploy services to commits whose pipeline passed and (when the environment requires it, or the branch is unprotected) were approved on the source server, from the repositories and branches this server allows. It holds against a `COMPONENT_DEPLOY` holder of this server, an `ADMIN` who uses the deploy services (System › Components), a stolen session or API key of one, and a network attacker (the secret is never sent, a changed byte fails the signature, an old attestation is stale, a replay is refused, an attestation for another target is refused). It does not hold against whoever holds the secret, within the allow list: the source server (its database key and the environment's encrypted token; a compromised source or a maintainer who reads its database deploys what it signs), anyone who reads this server's `runtime/.env`; nor against whoever edits this server's conf (sealed `CONF_EDIT`) or files, who can turn the gate off or place a component for the next start; nor against whoever writes this server's database (an `ADMIN` with Tools' SQL and Allow Changes, Data Load or a record editor), who can change `DeployedComponent`, the attestation record or a component's `db` repository that a start or another member reads. A source signs only after the run passed, for a branch the environment allows, and after `decide#GitDeployment` by an approver other than the requester when approval is required; its run check still decides the run's trust. One secret per target still: the audience check stops an attestation for one target being taken by another, but two targets that share a secret share whatever one of them leaks.

## Hosted instances

System › Instances (`System/Instances.xml` and its tabs: `InstanceList`, `InstanceDetail`, `Environments`, `Hosts`, `Images`, `DatabaseHosts`, `Kinds`) provisions and runs other instances (MR Moqui, Postgres, MySQL, OpenSearch) as containers and makes their databases, through `org.moqui.dev.InstanceServices` (`runtime/components/base/framework/service/org/moqui/dev/InstanceServices.xml`, Rust in `crates/moqui-instance`; [Run-Deploy, Managed instances](../Run-Deploy.md#managed-instances)).

- **Gate.** The screens are under the `SYSTEM_APP` screen authorization. Every `InstanceServices` service (not the CI `RunDocker` ones) refuses a caller without the sealed `INSTANCE_ADMIN` permission, granted to `ADMIN_ADV`, unless authorization is disabled (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-02, fixed). The transitions that call a service are `method="post"`. The pages read rows only: nothing reaches a host as a page renders.
- **No secret in a row.** A host's TLS client key, a registry login and a database host's admin login are operational vault items by binding (`InstanceHost:<id>:tlsClientKey`, `InstanceImage:<id>:registry`, `DatabaseHost:<id>:admin`), opened only under a lease an approver opened or an environment's machine access; the credentials a provision makes are deposited into the environment's vault, which the server cannot read back ([Vaults](Vaults.md#operational-vaults)). A provision that opens items is refused without a lease whose signed statement names that provision and the environment's vault, or the environment's machine access; the environment is the one the instance's row names, never a request's. No parameter, result, action report or log line holds a value.
- **Rows.** `InstanceHost`, `InstanceHostType`, `InstanceImage`, `InstanceKind` and its env, volume, host-config, secret and dependency rows, and `DatabaseHost` are restricted for the generic tools, REST and request loads (`is_host_config_entity`); `InstanceEnvironment`, `InstanceCluster`, `AppInstance` and its env, secret, dependency, volume and host-config rows, and `InstanceAction` are service-written (`is_service_written_entity`, `crates/moqui-entity/src/entity/sensitive.rs`): an instance's environment decides its machine access and its secret bindings which items a provision opens. The `store#` and `create#AppInstance` services write them, and seed data.
- **A service named by a row.** An action runs the services its host type names (`InstanceHostType`'s start, stop, remove and check services); each must be in `org.moqui.dev.InstanceServices`.
- **Docker Engine API.** The `docker` host type's services and the provision use the shared driver (`moqui_lib::docker::DockerHost`), at `InstanceHost.hostProtocol`, `hostAddress` and `adminPort` (default 2375): a unix socket, https with `tlsCaCert`, `tlsClientCert` and the leased client key, or plain http only to loopback (`localhost`, 127.0.0.0/8, `::1`, `::ffff:127.x`); plain http to any other host is refused before anything is sent (SEC-OUT-08). The seed has an `InstanceHost` `LocalDocker` at `http://127.0.0.1:2375`. A container takes its image, its settings (`AppInstanceEnv`), named volumes, the environment's network, a published port (bound to the host's loopback, never every address), a restart policy and a health check; no host path, host network or engine socket (the driver refuses them), and no secret in its environment, labels, command or entrypoint (`ContainerSpec::check` refuses it before anything is sent): each secret is a file under `/run/secrets`, 0400. The request is also in [Requests the server makes](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md#requests-the-server-makes).
- **Where a login goes.** The operation a provision's lease names, which the server signs and the approver reads, lists each leased item with its destination; at its use the item's own destination is checked against the row (a `database-credential` item's `host` and `port`, a `registry-login` item's `registry`), and an image with a registry login must pull from its `registryLocation`, since `X-Registry-Auth` goes to the registry its name says. A mismatch is refused before the login is sent (`destinationMismatch`, `registryMismatch`). An environment that names an approver group takes actions only from its members; one action runs under a lease at a time.
- **Templates.** A kind's env and volume templates fill only named placeholders (`${instanceName}`, `${databaseHost}` and the others of `kinds::PLACEHOLDERS`); any other `${}` is refused, so no template is script.
- **Database creation with an admin login.** On a database instance, the user and database are made by a one-shot client container of the server's image on its host and network, whose admin login and statements are secret files and whose command names only files; its logs are read for its own `moqui:` lines only. On a database host the manager connects with `verify-full` TLS (`crates/moqui-instance/src/database.rs`); the Postgres driver refuses to send a password in clear text over a connection without TLS (`pgwire.rs`) and the MySQL driver never enables the clear-text plugin. A Postgres user is made with a SCRAM-SHA-256 verifier, so the password is in no statement or log. Names must match `^[A-Za-z0-9_]+$`; a MySQL password with a quote or a backslash is refused (SEC-TLS-10). A user or database there already is refused, not reused. A provision that fails is undone (containers, volumes, the database it made, its deposits withdrawn) and a volume there already is refused, so an earlier instance's data is never taken over with new credentials.
- **CI runs.** The pipeline run services of the same file (`start#RunDocker` and the others, `moqui_git::ci::driver::docker`) use the same `InstanceHost` rows; a CI run is unattended, so an https runner host's client key is opened per call under the machine access of the environment the host names (`InstanceHost.environmentId`, `moqui_core::vault_hooks::machine_run`), and refused without it. They are in [Pipelines](Attack-Surface-Git.md#pipelines).

| `REPORT_CONTACT_ADMIN` | `org.moqui.report.contact.webhook`: alert webhooks |
| `REPORT_ALERT_EVALUATION` | `org.moqui.report.alert.evaluateAll`: the alert job evaluating every owner's rules |
| `REPORT_SHARE_ADMIN` | every `org.moqui.report.share.*`: sharing a report definition with a group the sharer is not in ([Sharing](../tools/Monitoring.md#sharing)) |

| `REPORT_BUSINESS_ADMIN` | `org.moqui.report.annotation.business` and every `org.moqui.report.rows.*`: business annotations, and the records of a dataset whose definition restricts them |
| `REPORT_DELIVERY_ADMIN` | `org.moqui.report.delivery.group`: a schedule's recipients other than its owner, asked by `store#Schedule`, `create#Schedule` (for a `userId` other than the owner, or a group), `check#ReportAudience` and `find#ReportRecipients`, and again before a run that delivers to others renders, as the owner (`GROUP_DELIVERY_DENIED`, `crates/moqui-report/src/schedule.rs`; SEC-TLS-105) |

A report query is authorized on its dataset (`org.moqui.report.dataset.<id>` by the caller's own grant, plus `DataDocumentUserGroup` for a DataDocument) as the viewer, or as the owner for alerts and scheduled reports, with server-side row filters. Two parts of a query read past that, with authorization off: a field's `lookup` in the dataset's `fieldDefaults` (an entity, key field, display field and conditions the dataset's author writes), whose rows are answered as the field's mappings, limited to the rows the frame's ids name (the key is the entity's single primary-key field, the conditions plain equalities) and to fields that are neither password, encrypted nor `UserAccount` identity fields (SEC-TLS-82), and a registered scope policy service ([Datasets and queries](#report-files-alerts-and-git-sync)). The permissions `MONITOR_INGEST` (the owner of an ingest key), `MONITOR_REPLAY`, `MONITOR_INCIDENT`, `MONITOR_RECOVER`, `MONITOR_RECOVER_ADMIN`, `MONITOR_SETTINGS`, `MONITOR_PRIVATE_NETWORK` and `REPORT_SAVE_LOCATION` (scheduled report files on the server) are seeded on `ADMIN` in this file. `MONITOR_EVAL` and `MONITOR_EVAL_RUN` are seeded on `ADMIN` in `runtime/components/base/framework/data/AgentEvalData.xml`.

### Report files, alerts and git sync

- **Datasets and queries.** `/rest/s1/moqui/reports/query`, `/documents` and `/datasets` (`run#Query`, `query#Documents`, `list#Datasets`, `runtime/components/base/tools/service/moqui.rest.xml`) take the whole query from the caller (dataset, aggregations, filters, sort, documents mode, transformations), so the dataset's rules are the whole boundary; a saved definition restricts nothing (`crates/moqui-report/src/lib.rs`). A dataset is authorized by the caller's own grant, never an inherited one. Its row filter, the AND of its `rowFilter`, its `contextFilters` (values from the signed-in principal only) and the `filter` its `rowFilterService` answers, goes in before every request part, and no caller key removes it; `rows: "restricted"` is checked before documents, top hits and a drill's records. A query is held to 64 queries, aggregation nodes and steps, 10,000 service rows, 1,000 a page, a timeout of 100 ms to 60 s, ten years, and 65,536 rows a join. A drill takes no query, entity or service from the request; frame transformations are arithmetic and reduce names from a fixed list, and a filter regex is the linear `regex` crate. A dataset body carries authority of its own, which only a holder of the dataset-save artifact (`org.moqui.report.dataset`, update: `ADMIN`, and in mr-boss the steward group `BOSS_INSIGHTS_DATA`) writes: `serviceName`, a service every reader calls in process with the query's parameters (a dashboard panel's saved query carries `parameters` its author wrote), and `rowFilterService`, called with `userId`, `datasetId` and `rowFilterParameters`, each needing the reader's own `view` grant of the service; a registered scope policy is called with authorization off. A saved dataset names only a service a component's shipped dataset names in the same place (CalcGrid's `get#CalcFrame`), and as `rowFilterService` also a registered scope policy: `save#Definition` refuses any other, and so does each read of a dataset that is not shipped, whatever wrote its row (`definitions::service_refusal`, SEC-TLS-91); and `fieldDefaults.<field>.lookup`, an entity find with authorization off whose rows are answered as the field's mappings. A lookup's `keyField` is the entity's single primary-key field, so it reads only the rows whose ids are in the authorized frame, and its `conditions` are plain equalities; it never reads a restricted entity or a view over one (`is_restricted_entity_or_view`), and never keys, shows or conditions on a password or encrypted field (`generic_access`'s hidden fields) or an identity field of `UserAccount` (`USER_ACCOUNT_IDENTITY_FIELDS`, followed by origin as the password fields are, so a view's alias that renames one, through views of views, is one: `sensitive::identity_fields`; no list of usernames or email addresses). It still reads any other entity's display field for those ids (an organization's or product's name, as the shipped datasets do); `save#Definition` refuses a dataset with any other lookup, and a query leaves such a field's values unlabelled with one warning per dataset field (`crates/moqui-report/src/lookup.rs`, SEC-TLS-82). A telemetry alias opens on its owner's grant of the index, not the reader's, under the alias's `rowFilter`, while the owner holds it and the account is open (`crates/moqui-report/src/alias.rs`).
- **Business datasets.** `create#BusinessDataset` takes a `dataDocumentId` that exists, is in a data feed and that a group of the creator's reads (`DataDocumentUserGroup`, as a reader of the document needs). `store#BusinessDatasetFields` and `store#BusinessDatasetConditions` then write the `DataDocumentField` and `DataDocumentCondition` rows of that document, with authorization off, and `index#BusinessDataset` rebuilds its index (`crates/moqui-report/src/business.rs`, `check_document_steward`): the document is shared with other datasets, the app's own search screens and the feed, so a create and a change of its fields or conditions need the steward's groups to read it and the steward to steward every other dataset over it (by its document, its id or its document's index, shipped or saved; the refusal counts them and names none), and a field or condition is changed or removed only by the dataset that added it (`BusinessDataset.fieldSeqIds`, `conditionSeqIds`, cleared when a dataset is made), never one made with the document or in Tools (SEC-TLS-92). An index rebuild needs only the dataset's steward: it widens nothing. A new field is a relationship path of up to six names whose last is not a secret name or an encrypted field, and a field a report definition names is not removed. A new or changed field, of the document's own entity too, needs every group that reads the document to be one the steward may widen (`may_widen`, SEC-TLS-92): `ADMIN` passes, `ALL_USERS` never does, a group the steward is in passes, and any other only by `org.moqui.report.widen.<userGroupId>` (update) in the steward's own grant (`REPORT_WIDEN_ADMIN` gives `ADMIN` the pattern; mr-boss gives `BOSS_INSIGHTS_DATA` it for the Insights groups only). So a field a steward adds widens what the document's reader groups read from the index only for groups the steward may widen. The app's own search screens over a document the steward's groups read are not datasets and are not asked. Reached by a holder of the dataset-save artifact and `org.moqui.report.dataset.<id>` (update), through `Dashboards/Datasets`, `DatasetFields` and `DatasetDetail`, and in mr-boss through the Insights Report builder, which takes the same transitions in.
- **Owner transfer and recipients.** `transfer#DefinitionOwner`, `pause#OwnedSchedules`, `find#OwnedSchedules`, `transfer#AlertRuleOwner`, `pause#OwnedAlertRules` and `find#OwnedAlertRules` (`crates/moqui-report/src/delivery.rs`, `alert_delivery.rs`) act on the caller's own resources, or on another user's with `REPORT_OWNER_CHANGE` (`ADMIN` in `framework/data/ReportSetupData.xml`; in mr-boss the identity operator and the administrator, called by its offboarding through `ironstone.admin.LifecycleParticipant` rows); an occurrence list beyond one's own needs `ADMIN_TRANSFER_VIEW`. No Tools screen calls them. `transfer#DefinitionOwner` re-owns the schedule's definition and every dashboard, report, panel, dataset, CalcGrid or search definition it references that the leaving owner owns, and pauses the schedule; it neither moves nor walks through an alert rule, a contact point, a silence or a mute timing (`MOVES_WITH_SCHEDULE`), so a rule stays its author's until `transfer#AlertRuleOwner`; the new owner's qualification leaves those definitions out too, so a schedule whose dashboard names its leaving author's rule is handed on (`transfer_scope_as`, `scope_reading`); `transfer#AlertRuleOwner` pauses a rule until the new owner activates it. A new owner must be an active internal user who may store schedules, hold group delivery for a group schedule and read every source. `get#RecipientReport` (`Dashboards/ReportDelivery` `ownReportFile`) renders a delivered report again as the recipient and releases nothing when the recipient's scope hash differs from the delivery's. `check#ReportAudience` answers, for any user id or group named, the account's username and full name and a reason code to a holder of `org.moqui.report.delivery.group` (create), and the caller only to anyone else. `find#ReportRecipients` lists active internal accounts by a term to such a holder; the engine's `audience` decides, and it takes any group id.
- **Scheduled report files.** A report schedule with `saveToLocation` writes each rendered file (`pdf`, `png`, `csv`, `xlsx` or `html`) to that resource location when it runs (`crates/moqui-report/src/schedule.rs`). The creator needs `REPORT_SAVE_LOCATION`, now sealed, when the schedule is saved, and the owner is checked again at each run. The location must be under a root of the conf's `report` `save-roots` (`report@save-roots`, default `dbresource://report/`), with no `..` segment, no encoded separator or backslash, not `component:`, no `conf` segment and no `.env` file (`context/save_location.rs` `report_save_location_problem`): `create#Schedule` refuses another (`schedule.rs` `check_save_location`) and the delivery checks each file again. The older `run#ScreenScheduled` save checks the same and no longer expands the location as a template (plans/Security-Review.md SEC-TLS-06, fixed); mr-marble's My Account › Scheduled Finds edit (`updateScreenScheduled`) sets only the schedule's own fields (`renderMode`, `noResultsAbort`, `cronExpression`, `fromDate`, `thruDate`, `emailTemplateId`, `emailSubject`), never `saveToLocation`, `screenPath`, `userGroupId` or the user. Parent directories are made, and an existing file is replaced (`write_bytes`, `crates/moqui-core/src/context/resource.rs`). With more than one file the table's name is added before the extension. `store#Schedule` reaches only the caller's own report schedule at the revision read and always clears `saveToLocation` and `userGroupId`, so a schedule it saves writes no file. What an author sets in a saved file is text, never markup: every CSV header and cell is quoted with quotes doubled and a text cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a return gets a leading `'`; an HTML file is a fixed prologue with the title and commentary escaped and the data as JSON in a script with `<` escaped; the file name is the panel title reduced to letters, digits, `-` and `_`. So a saved file cannot be made into a screen, a script or a conf file by its author.
- **Alert rules.** `org.moqui.report.ReportServices.evaluate#Alerts` with no `definitionId` needs `org.moqui.report.alert.evaluateAll` (update) of a signed-in caller with authorization on: `authorize_own` answers yes when authorization is disabled or no context is entered (`crates/moqui-core/src/user/authz.rs`), so the job passes the check without holding it. With a `definitionId` it needs no evaluation grant: any reader of the rule (its owner, or a member of its share group) starts an evaluation, bounded by the rule's interval (60 seconds at least) and its claim. It loads the rules with authorization off and evaluates each in a new context signed in as the rule's owner (`internal_login_user`, `crates/moqui-report/src/alert.rs`). A contact point is `notification`, `email` or `webhook`. An `email` contact sends the rule's payload through the template named to the `toAddresses` text stored in the rule. A `webhook` contact needs `org.moqui.report.contact.webhook` (update) at delivery. Its URL is `http://` or `https://` with no user info and no query, or comes from a server property named in `urlProperty`. Literal headers are refused, and header values come only from `MONITOR_WEBHOOK_*` or `monitor.webhook.*` properties. The POST has a 10 second timeout and refuses internal addresses ([Requests the server makes](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md#requests-the-server-makes)). A `notification` contact is checked at save and asked again as the rule's owner at each send (`contact_refusal`, `contact_refusal_as`, `crates/moqui-report/src/alert.rs`; SEC-TLS-106): a `userId` other than the owner needs the group delivery grant, a `userGroupId` must pass `may_share_with`, and the `topic` must be a `NotificationTopic` with `reportAlertTopic` Y (the framework's `ReportAlert`, or an app's own in its data); an audience whose topic is no longer registered at the send has its in-app recipients skipped. The notification carries the rule's title and its `detailLink`, a server path. A rule or series labelled `incident="true"` opens a `MonitorIncident` when it fires only when its owner is an active account holding `MONITOR_INCIDENT` by its own grants (`may_open_incident`), its service taken from the labels and its severity minor, major or critical, else major (`severity_of`, `crates/moqui-report/src/incident.rs`); an incident with no regression takes no automatic system-changing step (SEC-TLS-107). Each rule is evaluated signed in as its owner through the login gate (`internal_login_user` refuses a disabled, terminated or password-expired account); a technical rule whose owner cannot sign in fails and is logged, a business one is paused. On the demo `email-client allowed-hosts="localhost,127.0.0.1"` (`runtime/components/dev/demo/MoquiConf.xml`) holds an `email` contact's mail to an SMTP host on the demo machine or the deployment's own edge node.
- **Git sync.** A `moqui.report.DefinitionGitSync` row ties a definition folder to a directory of a repository this server hosts (`git://<repositoryId>/<path>/`, `crates/moqui-report/src/git_sync.rs`). Creating, listing, pushing and pulling one by hand needs `org.moqui.report.gitSync` (update); `Dashboards/GitSync.xml` lists every `GitRepository` with authorization off for its drop-down, shown only to such a holder. A sync's `repositoryId` and `path` are as its creator gives them: the `git://` resource scheme is not checked against the repository's `AT_GIT_REPOSITORY` access (`crates/moqui-git/src/lib.rs`), so the grant writes definition files into, and reads `*.json` files from, any hosted repository. A run-as `userId` other than the caller needs `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` and an account `user::held_beyond` names nothing of, `DefinitionGitSync.storedByUserId` records the storer, and each push and pull runs only while the storer still holds `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` and `held_beyond` names nothing, asked as the storer (`as_sync_user`, SEC-TLS-109). A push or a fetch to the repository pulls (`pull#DefinitionGitSyncs`, from the git ECA, which checks no permission): each changed file is saved as a definition, and a tracked definition whose file is gone is deleted, in a context signed in as the sync's user. A pulled file goes through the same `save` as `save#Definition`: the user must own an existing definition, a share group not already stored needs `may_share_with`, a dataset the user's own dataset-save grant, and every reference must be readable; a business alert rule not already active arrives paused with no contact point. So whoever may push to that repository writes report definitions with the sync user's rights, and a push writes into the repository every definition of the folder the sync user may read.

### Monitor screens and telemetry

The receiver, RUM and metrics routes are rows of [Host routes and servlets](Attack-Surface-Routes.md#host-routes-and-servlets). What the Monitor screens and their services add:

- **What spans and logs hold.** A request's SERVER span holds the method, `url.path` (a token route's token left out), `url.query`, the `Host`, the client address, the user agent, the `Referer`, the route, the status, the first error message of the request, and the session's `session.id` (a digest of the bound session id, never the id itself, SEC-TLS-18), `user.id`, `moqui.visitor.id` and `moqui.visit.id` (`crates/moqui-host/src/http.rs`). It holds no request body and no form parameter. In `url.query` a value is `[REDACTED]` when `telemetry::sensitive_key` takes its parameter name as a secret: a credential parameter name (`password`, `api_key`, `login_key` and the others of `web::CREDENTIAL_PARAMETER_NAMES`), `code`, `moquisessiontoken`, `sessiontoken`, `sql`, `sharedsecret`, a name that contains `password`, `secret`, `token`, `authorization`, `cookie`, `credential`, `arguments`, `locals`, `api.key`, `api-key`, `apikey` or `private_key`, or one that ends in `_key`, `-key`, `.key` or `loginkey` (`crates/moqui-core/src/context/identity.rs`). Of the other parameters `url.query` and the Referer's query keep the name and, by default, the value only of `pageIndex`, `pageSize`, `orderByField`, `renderMode`, `lastStandalone` and `_uiType`, the others `[…]`, and the Referer no fragment; `<telemetry query-values="all">` keeps every value, for debugging (`http::redacted_query`, `redacted_referer`; SEC-TLS-18). A span attribute or a log attribute with a secret name is dropped. A log record holds its level, logger, thread, `user.id` and `moqui.visitor.id`, and a body of at most 16 KiB. The SQL text of a span and the pattern that masks a secret in a text value are in [Logs and spans](Attack-Surface-Data.md#logs-and-spans). The same rules run on received OTLP attributes (128 a record, 16 KiB a value) before indexing. The telemetry datasets have no row filter: a reader of `otel_spans`, `otel_logs` or `otel_errors` reads every user's. DEBUG and TRACE events are not exported (`crates/moqui-host/src/logging.rs`). Head sampling is decided from the trace id and the configured ratio, never from a caller's flag; the tail rule keeps an unsampled root that has an exception or is slower than `slow-root-ms`; a producer never waits on the queue (`try_send`) (`crates/moqui-core/src/telemetry/sdk.rs`, `telemetry/mod.rs`).
- **Scrape targets and synthetic checks.** Monitor › Checks (`System/Monitor/Checks.xml`) stores `moqui.monitor.ScrapeTarget` and `moqui.monitor.SyntheticCheck` rows through entity-auto services. Both entities are members of `SYSTEM_APP`, so no permission beyond the group is asked. The jobs `scrape_TelemetryTargets_minute` and `run_SyntheticChecks_minute` run each enabled row whose interval has passed, and Scrape now and Run now call `org.moqui.monitor.TelemetryServices.scrape#Targets` and `run#Checks` for one row at once. A scrape is a `GET` of the row's `url` with `Authorization: Bearer` from `bearerToken` (an encrypted field) when set; a write of a new `url`, by any path but a seed or install load, clears the stored `bearerToken` unless a new one is written in the same save (`Security.eecas.xml` `ScrapeTargetSecretMoves`, `admin/EndpointSecrets.js`; SEC-TLS-116). A check is a `GET`, `HEAD` or `POST` with no headers and no body. The URL must be `http` or `https`. Both send with `deny_internal: false` (`crates/moqui-core/src/telemetry/scrape.rs`), so loopback, private and link-local addresses are reached ([The internal-address guard](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md#the-internal-address-guard)). The timeout is the row's `timeoutSeconds`, 10 by default and 60 at most. A scrape body over 8 MiB is refused after it is read, and at most 10,000 points a scrape are written to `otel_metrics` under `source.id` `scrape:<targetId>`. A scrape keeps only lines that parse as Prometheus samples, and the row's `lastError` holds the transport error or `HTTP <status>`. A check compares the status with `expectStatus` and, when `expectBodyContains` is set, searches the body for it; the row's `lastMessage` and a span hold the result.
- **Ingest keys.** Monitor › Sources makes an ingest key with `org.moqui.monitor.MonitorServices.create#IngestKey`, which needs `MONITOR_INGEST`. The token is `mqi_` and 32 random bytes in URL-safe base64 (47 characters). Only its SHA-256 is stored, as `IngestKey.keyId`, and a request's key is found by that hash (`crates/moqui-report/src/monitor.rs`). The key expires after `expireDays` (90 by default, 3,650 at most). The token is shown once, in a public message of the page after the post: the message travels in the response's `messages` and stays in the tab's notification history (the last 25, in page memory) until a reload; no write of that history to browser storage was found. `list#IngestKeys` and `revoke#IngestKey` reach only the caller's own keys: an administrator does not see or revoke another user's key there. A request is refused when the key is revoked or expired, when its owner cannot be signed in (`internal_login_user`), or when its owner does not hold `MONITOR_INGEST`. A token that is not `mqi_` and 47 characters is refused before any read. The key is taken only from the `api_key` or `Authorization: Bearer` header, never from the query.
- **What a key's sender sets.** The receiver forces `source.id` (the key's hash) and `source.name` (the key's label) on every record. Every other field is the sender's: `service.name`, `service.version`, `service.instance.id`, `user.id`, `moqui.visit.id`, `session.id`, `client.address` and any other mapped field are taken from the resource and record attributes (`crates/moqui-core/src/telemetry/ingest.rs`). A record that names this server's own `service.name` (in its resource, or in a record or data point attribute) is kept as another service's: the receiver's decoder (`Decoder::receiving`) makes it `<name> (received)`, so the regression detector, the release list and verification, which select by `service.name` (`crates/moqui-report/src/regression/mod.rs`), never read a sender's record as the local service's; the `resource` map keeps what was sent (SEC-TLS-14). `POST /v1/annotations` calls `create_annotation` as the key's owner: the source is `deploy`, `config`, `release`, `user` or `business`, with `serviceName`, `instanceId`, `version` and `revision` from the body; `created_by` is the owner and `ingest_source` the key's label. Through the receiver a `business` annotation is refused whoever owns the key (SEC-TLS-15), and so is a `release` whose `serviceName` is this server's own (SEC-TLS-14; `annotations.rs` `ingest_refusal`); the server records its own releases at start.
- **Runtime settings.** `System/Monitor/Settings.xml` changes settings in memory for 15, 60, 240 or 1440 minutes through `MonitorServices.set#RuntimeSetting`, `revert#RuntimeSetting` and `revert#AllRuntimeSettings`. Each needs `MONITOR_SETTINGS`, checked in the service (`crates/moqui-report/src/settings.rs`); `list#RuntimeSettings` needs only a signed-in user. A change needs a reason, is published on the `runtime-settings` cluster topic, is logged on `moqui.monitor.audit` and is written as a `config` annotation. The settings are the log levels (global, console, and a rule for any target; `trace` only on a target rule), `telemetry@sample-ratio` and `slow-root-ms`, spans and metrics per artifact type, the attribute list of each instrument (32 names at most), `telemetry/operations`, `telemetry/browser` (`enabled`, `replay-enabled` and the sample ratios), and `telemetry/receiver` (`enabled`, `requests-per-minute` up to 1,000,000, `body-limit` up to 64 MiB) (`crates/moqui-core/src/live_settings/catalog.rs`). So a holder turns browser RUM, session replay and the OTLP receiver on or off for a running server, and changes the receiver's limits, without a conf change.
- **Recovery.** A `RecoveryPolicy` matches an open incident by a service-name pattern and least severity, and its ladder names actions: `observe`, `escalate`, `pause-alert-rule`, `pause-job`, `disable-artifact`, `isolate-instance`, `pause-rollout`, `promote`, `restart`, `rollback`. The in-process adapter does four itself (`crates/moqui-report/src/recovery/execute.rs`): `pause-job` sets `paused` `Y` on the named `ServiceJob`; `pause-alert-rule` sets `paused` in the named report definition; `disable-artifact` creates an `ArtifactAuthz` row `RECOVERY_<actionId>` that denies the named artifact group to `ALL_USERS` for all actions; `isolate-instance` drains the named instance. `restart`, `rollback`, `promote` and `pause-rollout` are carried out only by a webhook or by an operator; no action runs a command. An action is started when a `MONITOR_RECOVER` holder approves its decision, or by itself under an `automatic` policy with `telemetry/recovery` `allow-automatic="true"`, and not while the emergency stop is on; `escalate` always runs. The seed policy `DEFAULT` (advisory) escalates major and critical incidents to the `ADMIN` group. `store#RecoveryPolicy`, `store#RecoveryAdapter` and `set#RecoveryStop` need `MONITOR_RECOVER_ADMIN`; approve, refuse, complete, cancel and revert need `MONITOR_RECOVER`; the incident and release services need `MONITOR_INCIDENT`. `run#Recovery` and `detect#Regressions` check no permission. The job `run_Recovery_minute` calls `run#Recovery`, which, with authorization off, advances each `RecoveryAction` row whose status is `Persisted`, `Running` or `AwaitingOperator` (`recovery/mod.rs`, `execute::advance`). Before each attempt it reads the decision again (`refused_by_decision`): it must exist, match the action's incident, action, target and adapter, and be approved (or blocked, for an action that changes nothing, escalation); else the action fails without running. While the emergency stop is on, an action that changes the system waits (SEC-TLS-11, fixed). An incident with no regression, an operator's or one an alert rule's `incident` label opened, is decided at confidence 1.0, above any policy's `minConfidence` (`recovery/mod.rs`), but an automatic policy's step that changes the system waits for approval on it (`automatic_refusal`, `recovery/policy.rs`; SEC-TLS-107).
- **Recovery webhook adapters.** `store#RecoveryAdapter` refuses a `url` with credentials or a scheme other than `http` and `https`. It stores `statusUrl`, `urlProperty`, `secretProperty`, `headerPropertiesJson` and `allowInternal` as given, and the sender refuses a scheme other than `http` and `https`. The headers, the URL of `urlProperty` and the HMAC key are the values of process properties the adapter names (`moqui_lib::env::get_property`), each one starting with `recovery_` or listed in the conf property `telemetry/recovery@properties`; a URL or header property the conf marks secret needs the list too (the HMAC key may be secret: it is not sent). The store and each run check this (`execute::property_allowed`; SEC-TLS-12, fixed). No `recovery_` property is set in the shipped conf, so a deployment sets its own. The callback token is 32 random characters made with the action row, only its SHA-256 is stored, and it goes only with the first attempt; a controller that never got it cannot report on `/v1/recovery`, though its answer to a retry still sets the status, and the action otherwise ends at its deadline. `recovery/verify.rs` reads only the incident's regressions, the kept baseline and the metrics since the action, nothing from a request. The route row is in [Host routes and servlets](Attack-Surface-Routes.md#host-routes-and-servlets).
- **Monitor rows and the generic tools.** The `MONITOR_*` permissions are checked in the Monitor services. The recovery entities (`RecoveryAction`, `RecoveryAdapter`, `RecoveryPolicy`, `RecoveryControl`), `InstanceDrain`, `IngestKey`, `ScrapeTarget` and `SyntheticCheck` are service-written (`sensitive.rs` `SERVICE_WRITTEN_ENTITY_KEYS`), so entity REST, Service REST, Tools › Data › Record, the grid, ETL targets and loads refuse to write them; they are written only by Monitor's services (SEC-TLS-11). A scrape target's or synthetic check's `allowPrivateNetwork` set to `Y`, or a move of such a row's URL, needs `MONITOR_PRIVATE_NETWORK` (`store#ScrapeTarget`, `store#SyntheticCheck`; [Outbound](Attack-Surface-Outbound.md)). An `IngestKey` row is accepted by its `keyId`, `revoked`, expiry and its owner's `MONITOR_INGEST`. `MonitorIncident`, `MonitorIncidentEvidence`, `MonitorIncidentCause`, `MonitorRegression`, `RecoveryDecision` and `MonitorScopeLock`, and the report rows `moqui.report.Definition`, `DefinitionGitSync`, `DefinitionGitFile` and `BusinessDataset`, are not service-written: Tools › Data, a load and Service Run write them, and the engine trusts a definition's `ownerId`, a sync's `userId` and `storedByUserId` and a business dataset's `admittedDate`. Tools › Data › Load sets `restrictSensitiveEntities(true)`, so a load refuses the service-written entities and takes these, and so does its `storeSelected`.
- **Session replays.** `System/Monitor/ReplayDetail.xml` `events` is a `read-only` transition that answers a replay's rrweb events as JSON. It and `MonitorServices.list#BrowserReplays` and `get#BrowserReplay` each check `MONITOR_REPLAY`. `get#BrowserReplay` writes one INFO line (`session replay viewed`, with the replay and the viewer) on the ordinary log target, not on `moqui.monitor.audit`; listing is not logged. The report engine refuses the `browser_replays` index as a dataset: by id, by `index:` alias and as the target of a saved dataset (`crates/moqui-report/src/lib.rs`). The server stores a chunk's events as the page sent them, after removing token-route tokens; masking is done only in the browser. `/rum/replay` takes chunks from any signed-in user while `telemetry/browser@replay-enabled` is on (off by default), at 120 posts a minute for a user and visit and 1 MiB a body; the size and time caps hold for one `replayId`, which the page chooses (16 to 64 letters, digits and dashes), and `replay-sample-ratio` is applied by the page only. Each user's replay bytes are also held to `telemetry/browser@replay-user-bytes-per-hour` (50 MiB) on each member, for at most 4,096 users an hour, the least recently active evicted past that (SEC-TLS-110). The replay budget map (`crates/moqui-report/src/browser.rs`) is one process's memory and holds 4,096 replays; after an eviction, a restart or on another member it makes a new entry with whoever posts first as the owner. A chunk's document `_id` is `<replayId>-<owner tag>-<seq>`, the tag made from the poster's user and visit, so another poster's chunks of the same `replayId` are rows of their own; `get#BrowserReplay` reads only the session of the replay's first chunk, and `list#BrowserReplays` lists each replay once, as its first-received chunk of sequence 0 (`first_chunks`). The qvt client plays the events with rrweb's `Replayer`, the vendored `rrweb@2.1.7`, made with no `UNSAFE_replayCanvas` and no plugin.

### Co-editing

A wiki page is co-edited over the notification socket (`/notws`, [Host routes and servlets](Attack-Surface-Routes.md#host-routes-and-servlets)) by `collab` frames, beside the `calc` frames of CalcGrid rooms (`crates/moqui-host/src/notws/collab.rs`, `crates/moqui-collab/`).

- **Frames.** Any signed-in socket sends `{collab: join|update|awareness|leave, doc, client}`. `join` runs `moqui_collab::join`, which decodes the join's state vector before it counts the member (a refused join is no member) and asks `get#WikiPageAccess` for the socket's own user (view to join, update to edit); the answer is asked again each minute, a user who may no longer view is removed with `Revoked` and their clients dropped, a lost edit right is sent as a `canEdit` false frame, and a member is dropped once the answer is older than three minutes (`ACCESS_RECHECK`, `ACCESS_STALE`, `crates/moqui-collab/src/lib.rs`; SEC-TLS-112). A member admitted after its socket closed leaves at once, so a room is saved on its last editor's leave (SEC-TLS-111). The document key is `WikiPage:` and a plain id. `update` applies a Yjs update from a joined client of that socket with the edit right, up to the socket's 16 MiB message (refused over the cluster body limit), to the server's document; `awareness` passes any JSON on to every editor. A socket holds at most `webroot` `socket-room-clients` (4) clients of a room and `socket-awareness-bytes` (4096) of awareness, its joins are a token bucket of `socket-joins-per-minute` (60) and its user's of `socket-user-joins-per-minute` (240), a place is reserved before a join's work starts, and a document's state is at most `resource-facade@collab-max-state-bytes` (8 MiB) (SEC-TLS-113).
- **What is stored.** About 1.5 seconds after a change the room's tick writes the Yjs state and a markdown snapshot (`markdown::render`) to `moqui.resource.CollabDocument`, with the ids of the users whose edits are in no version. The tick saves a version through `update#WikiPage` as the room's last editor (`internal_login_user`) when no one is in the room, or after a quiet time for a page kept in the database. The markdown is text an editor may save anyway; text that would read as markdown structure is escaped, and nothing here makes HTML. An update may nest elements to any depth; the render writes 32 levels of elements and the plain text of what is deeper, read with a stack of its own, and the seed from a page's text opens at most 32 levels of quotes, lists and items (`MAX_DEPTH`, `markdown.rs`), so neither recurses past that on a worker's stack. The update is decoded by yrs at moqui's fork (docs/Build-Test.md Forks), which reads at most 128 levels of nested values (maps and arrays, in an attribute or embedded content) and refuses a deeper one as not an update (`Any::decode`), and reserves at most 1024 entries for a length an update or a state vector claims (SEC-TLS-117), in `join`, `apply` and `apply_remote` alike. Nested shared types are not bounded: deleting deeply nested ones recurses in yrs (SEC-TLS-118, open).
- **Services.** `org.moqui.resource.CollabServices.get#CollabDocument` checks view, then reads the row with authorization off and renders the open room; `save#CollabDocument` needs the edit right. mr-boss reaches them from `service/ironstone/commons/KnowledgeServices.xml`.

## Jobs

The scheduler is a thread in this process. `service-facade@scheduled-job-check-time` is 60 seconds. The first job pass waits 120 seconds, then every 60. `0` starts no job runner. Tick hooks such as the cluster heartbeat still run.

Scheduled cron jobs do not log in a user and do not run with artifact authorization. That is the scheduler's default. `dispatch` leaves `user_name` empty unless the row is an overflow queue (`ASYNC_QUEUE_`). `run_job` calls `internal_login_user` only when that name is set, then calls the service with authorization disabled. `ServiceJobUser` is who gets the completion or error notification. It is not the identity the job runs as. An explicit run-now logs in as the caller, and a queued fire-and-forget call logs in as the original caller, and both still disable authorization for the call. The tx-commit rules the scheduler runs from the outbox (`ServiceOutbox`, written in the committing transaction) log in as the user who made the call and run under the authorization the call had when it registered them, as on the commit hook; no service or screen reads the outbox, whose rows hold the call's parameters and results. One job's error is logged and recorded. It does not stop the scheduler.

Two worker paths turn authorization back on as the user. `run#EtlMapping` calls `etl::access::run_as_user` (`crates/moqui-tools/src/etl/access.rs`): it requires authorization again and pushes the Tools screen, checked for all actions as the user, so the run's writes and service calls run the entity ECAs and the privileged-group and sealed-permission rules. The `DatabaseLoadWrap` job does the same from script with `ec.artifactExecution.runAsUser(location, body)` (`tools_host.rs`). A cron run has no user: `run#EtlMapping` then signs in as the saved mapping's owner for that one run, so the owner's own permissions decide. The owner must be a `ServiceJobUser` of the job, and that row the owner's consent: added by the owner, or by a holder of `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` who may act as the owner (`held_beyond`), by `ServiceJobUser.addedByUserId`, which the `ServiceJobUserAddedBy` EECA sets at create and keeps on every update (`etl/service.rs` `sign_in_owner`, SEC-TLS-103); a row with no `addedByUserId` (a data load) refuses the run, and so does a mapping whose value maps are not its owner's. An `ADMIN` with `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS`, who may write both `ServiceJob` and `ServiceJobUser` (`System/Operations/JobDetail.xml` `createJobUser`), may therefore schedule another user's saved mapping to run as that user; that is allowed on purpose. A `DATABASE_ADMIN` holder who changes another user's mapping becomes its owner. Tools › ETL's own Schedule (`Tools/Etl.xml` `etlSchedule`, a POST that creates the `ServiceJob`, its parameter and its user through the entity-auto services and unpauses the job) makes the mapping's owner the job's user: for the caller's own mapping after storing it again, and for another user's only with `DATABASE_ADMIN`, `ADMIN_LOGIN_AS` and no access the owner holds beyond the caller (`Tools/Etl.xml:233-240`). Either way the job's `location` and `contentBytes` are ignored, so the run reads only the mapping's own source. The owner's login gate applies (a disabled or terminated account, or a password that must change or has expired, refuses the run), and an inline mapping is refused; the load wrap refuses a cron run. `run#DatabaseJob` on a worker re-checks `DATABASE_ADMIN` and runs the text of the job's `DatabaseJobStatement` rows as raw SQL, each on a connection of its own outside a transaction, with authorization off (`crates/moqui-tools/src/jobs.rs`): only a server-made plan writes those rows: the job entities are among those only their own services read and write (`is_audit_entity`).

`System/Operations/JobDetail.xml` changes a job two ways. `saveJob` and `runJob` call `org.moqui.admin.OperationServices.store#Job` and `execute#Recovery` (the delegated path). `updateJob`, `createJobParameter`, `updateJobParameter`, `deleteJobParameter`, the `ServiceJobUser` transitions and `runJobNow` are the instance administrator's: each has the `raw()` condition, and they write `moqui.service.job.ServiceJob` rows, `serviceName` included, through the entity-auto services. `runJobNow` calls `ec.service.job(jobName).run()`. A run-now signs in the caller and calls the job's service with authorization disabled, so a holder of these transitions runs any defined service with stored parameters under no artifact authorization; a permission the service checks itself is read from the caller. A job's run is a generic call (`generic`, `crates/moqui-core/src/service/jobs.rs`), so an entity-auto service it names is refused where a generic write is, as from Service Run; and `ServiceJob`, `ServiceJobParameter` and `ServiceJobUser` are among the entities only their own calls write (`SERVICE_WRITTEN_ENTITY_KEYS`), so the generic tools, REST and request loads write no job a cron runs.

### The embedded database backup

`backup#EmbeddedDatabase` (`org.moqui.impl.ServerServices`, Rust, `crates/moqui-core/src/service/backup.rs`, `authenticate="false"`) is the service the job `backup_EmbeddedDatabase_daily` runs, and the one that takes parameters: `backupDir` (else `database-backup@dir`, else `runtime/db/backup`), `systemMessageRemoteId` (the mirror remote; `EmbeddedDbBackup` in the seed), `mirrorTransport`, `mirrorEncrypt` and `mirrorRecipient`. System › Data › Backups is a view of it that takes none ([Backups](Attack-Surface-Admin.md#backups)). Whoever writes the job's `ServiceJobParameter` rows (the raw transitions of `System/Operations/JobDetail.xml`) or the mirror remote (`System/Integrations/ConnectionDetail.xml` `updateMessageRemote`), or calls the service by name (Tools › Service Run), chooses them; no permission is read on this path, and `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` is asked only by the data writer.

- **What runs on the host.** `pg_dump`, `pg_basebackup` and `pg_verifybackup` from the cluster's own `bin_dir`, each as `Command::new(bin).args(args)` with no shell and the superuser password in `PGPASSWORD`, and `kill -KILL -<pid>` on a timeout (`crates/moqui-drivers/src/backup.rs`). No argument comes from a request but the backup directory and the manifest path under it. The directory may not hold or sit inside a cluster's data directory. A dump is `0600` before `pg_dump` writes it, a physical backup `0700` and `0600`.
- **What a run deletes.** Every entry ending `.partial` in the backup directory at the start of a run (a directory with `remove_dir_all`), and every archive the retention plan drops; with a `backupDir` parameter that is in any directory the server can write.
- **The mirror.** The destination is the remote's `sendUrl` (an SFTP host, or `s3://bucket?region=&endpoint=`), the credentials its `username` and `password` or `privateKey` and `publicKey`, the directory or key prefix the message type's `sendPath` (`service/backup.rs`, `backup_store.rs`). It refuses a remote with `disabled` Y, warns when neither the mirror nor the local dumps are encrypted, keeps SFTP and S3 off internal addresses unless the row's `allowPrivateNetwork` allows the private network, and takes an `http://` S3 `endpoint` only for this machine or a private address on an instance that is not production (`backup_store.rs` `plain_endpoint_refusal`; SEC-TLS-96). An archive is sent plain unless `mirrorEncrypt` and an age recipient are set. The mirror also renames and deletes remote objects whose names parse as archive names, and its tar follows no link. SFTP checks the host key: the pinned `sshHostKeyFingerprint`, else trust on first use (`crates/moqui-core/src/sftp.rs`).
- **Download and restore.** No service, transition or REST resource downloads or restores an archive. Restore is the command line (`moqui load-save`) and the desktop app's tray, both a local user's. `load-save` restores the chosen dump into `moqui`, then every file beside it named `<name><suffix>` (letters, digits and `_`) into a database of that name, made if missing, with `pg_restore --clean` as the superuser; only `moqui` and `postgres` are left out (`crates/moqui-drivers/src/embedded.rs`).
- **The desktop app** (`crates/moqui-desktop/src/backup.rs`) backs up from a tray menu of the local user, with a settings file `backups` in the app's config directory, the default folder `MR-Moqui Backups` in Documents, and `moqui save-db` and `moqui load-save` as child processes. `save-db`'s dump is made `0600` before `pg_dump` writes it (`private_dump`), and the app makes a new backup folder private (`private_fs::create_dir_all`; SEC-TLS-97).
